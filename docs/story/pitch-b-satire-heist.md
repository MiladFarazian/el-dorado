# PITCH B — Satire-Forward Heist Comedy
**"ALL HAT"** — Ocean's Eleven swagger, Rockstar teeth, Texas manners.
*Story pitch, v1 (late July 2026). Built on canon: dfw-geography.md, dfw-culture.md, satire-targets.md, gta-design-dna.md.*

---

## 1. Working Title + Alternates

**Working title: ALL HAT** — from the local insult "all hat, no cattle." It describes the marks (fake cowboys, fake wealth, fake abundance) and the crew (professional wearers of other people's hats). One phrase that holds the whole thesis: in the Megaplex, everything is a costume, and the best-dressed liar wins.

- **Alt 1: THE HIGH FIVE** — the crew's name, taken from the five-level interchange where they scatter after the betrayal. Doubles as the box-art image and the sequel-able brand.
- **Alt 2: HOWDY STREET** — the financial-district heist framing; sells "Y'all Street" satire on the title screen.

**Parody city names** — canon (satire-targets.md) has these provisional; this pitch adopts them: Dallas → **Dorado**, Fort Worth → **Fort Verde**, the Metroplex → **the Megaplex**, Frisco → **Brisco**, Plano → **Blando**, Arlington → **Middlington**, Celina → **Salvation**, Southlake → **Shorelake**, Granbury → **Hollow Creek**. Proposed additions for places canon hasn't settled:
- Oak Cliff → **Oak Bluff** (a bluff is terrain AND a con — free theme)
- Deep Ellum → **Deep Bellum** (war under the murals)
- The Stockyards → **The Droveyards**
- Las Colinas → **Las Espejas** ("the mirrors" — a master-planned city of mirrored glass)
- Mesquite → **Catclaw** (thornier Texas shrub; rodeo-town grit; the protagonist's hometown)

---

## 2. Logline

A rodeo clown's daughter and her crew of Texas grifters rob the Megaplex's new untouchables — AI barons, prosperity pastors, private-equity landlords — until they discover who's really fencing their scores, and bet everything on one tornado-day heist.

---

## 3. Era and Season

**Right now: one full Texas year, August 2026 → May 2027.** Three reasons:

1. **The targets are perishable.** The Texchange rang its first cattle-horn bell in July 2026; the grid is forecast to nearly double demand for data centers; the crypto-mine noise suits are in federal court; the HQ-relocation count is 111 and climbing. This satire has a sell-by date — set it at the peak of the boom, not after the crash.
2. **The Texas calendar IS the heist calendar.** August heat dome and Conservation Appeals (Act 1 texture) → the Lone Star State Fair, late Sept–mid Oct (Act 1 climax) → megachurch Christmas pageant and HS-football playoff December (Act 2) → January Stock Show and an ice-storm low point → April–May supercell season (finale). Every act break is a weather system or a civic ritual the region actually runs on.
3. **Weather is the crew's sixth member.** In DFW, sirens empty streets on schedule, hail reprices every car on the lot, and a Conservation Appeal turns off the lights for you. The season structure makes the sky a planning tool — no other setting hands a heist writer this.

## 4. Protagonist + Crew

### Cassidy "Cass" Wynn, 33 — the Face
- **Background:** Catclaw (Mesquite-alike, "Rodeo Capital"), east side, working class. Daughter of Royce Wynn, rodeo pickup man and clown, and a mother who ran midway games at the fair — so Cass grew up fluent in the two native Texas arts: controlling where a crowd looks, and taking its money legally-ish. Trick rider by 14, repo agent by 22 (the license that teaches you every gated community's gate code), full-time grifter by 26. The family lost the arena lease and the land under it to a "trusted brother" from church running a crypto-mining Ponzi — machines that never existed, guaranteed 3–5% monthly (straight from canon's verified affinity-fraud pattern). He served 18 months and came back as a redemption-arc podcaster. Royce died patching roofs in hail season. Cass concluded the con is the region's true native industry, and decided to practice it *upward*.
- **Want vs. need:** She WANTS the one score big enough to buy back the Wynn arena land in Catclaw — restore the thing that was taken, name intact. She NEEDS to stop performing: to be known by five people instead of admired by a mark, and to learn that you can't con your way back to something that's gone — you can only protect what's still standing. The finale choice operationalizes this (§5).
- **Playstyle identity:** social-stealth first. Disguise and wardrobe with a literal **authenticity stat** (canon: locals clock boot wear, hat crease, truck-bed scratches — pristine cowboy fits fail infiltrations at the Droveyards but ace them in Brisco). Signature ability, **"the Lean"**: a read-the-room focus mode that surfaces marks, tells, exits, and camera cones. Signature physical verb from pickup-man childhood: **vehicle-to-vehicle transfer at speed** — the mechanical spine of three set pieces. Guns are a failure state; charm is the weapon wheel.
- **Why players will love inhabiting her:** she is the competence fantasy of Ocean's with Texas manners — "sir" and "ma'am" as lockpicks, "bless your heart" as a knife. Every conversation is a playground, every dress code is a puzzle, and the game rewards being *interesting* over being lethal. She's funny on purpose and the world is funny by accident, and she knows it.

### The crew — the High Five (systemic companion layer, per GTA-DNA solo-dev rule: one playable lead, crew as systems)

Each member is a satire target's collateral damage, recruited by robbing the thing that wronged them — so the roster IS the argument.

- **Priya "Patch" Anand, 29 — Tech.** Irving corridor, first-gen ambition, cricket-league family. Ex-OmniMind support lead who spent eleven months training the chatbot that replaced her, then got laid off *by* it (the termination call was summarized by Skye). Wants revenge on the machine; needs to admit she loved the work, not the company. In play: she runs Skye against its makers — every OmniMind product in the world is a door she can hold open. Recruitment mission: hijacking her own layoff livestream.
- **Dre "Elbows" Mercer, 26 — Wheels.** Oak Bluff, third-generation Boulevard. Slab prodigy, grandson of a Jefferson Boulevard lowrider-club president; can make a candy-paint donk disappear in traffic by going *slower* than everyone else. Wants his granddad's seized '63 out of the impound before auction; needs to stop treating the club's honor code as a cage — it's the only law in the game worth keeping. Club jobs are no-gun jobs, and the game's best driving missions inherit the rule.
- **Denny Hobbs, 41 — the Voice.** Ex-Overflow Fellowship campus pastor, casualty of the payout war: his satellite campus was "sunset" to fund the settlement while the jet stayed. Lost the faith, kept the stagecraft — fog cues, wireless-mic confidence, the ability to make 4,000 strangers feel personally seen. Wants to expose the machine; needs to forgive himself for having built it. His Act 2 arrest is the story's real cost.
- **Miss Lurlene Odom, 74 — Fence and mentor.** Did I-30-style condo land flips before lunch in 1985, when Dorado was the S&L fraud capital of the known world; served her time, kept her Rolodex. Now runs an antiques booth at the First Monday mega-flea, where anything can be bought and nothing has a receipt. Wants one last elegant score; needs an heir. Her thesis, and the game's: "Honey, we *invented* this. They just added an app."

**Crew mechanics:** each member carries a trust meter fed by hangouts and job conduct (GTA 5's visible-crew-competence DNA, systematized). Low-trust crew members fail loudly mid-heist — Patch's spoof drops, Elbows clips a gantry, Denny's voice cracks on the vault phone. Max-trust versions improvise saves the player didn't script. No switching tech; the crew is AI + barks + plan choices, priced for a solo dev.

## 5. Three-Act Arc

### Act 1 — ALL HAT (Aug–Oct 2026)
Cold open at the Catclaw rodeo: Cass mid-hustle as a trick rider (diegetic tutorial — ride, drive, charm, Lean — zero tutorial smell, per GTA-DNA onboarding rules). Her mark turns out to be a Bolo Capital asset manager there to buy the arena; she lifts his term sheet instead of his watch and reads the future in it: everything she's from is being rolled up.

- **Heat-dome texture:** Conservation Appeals dim the streetlights; the rich neighborhoods hum with generators; Big Merle reads grid notices in his concert-giveaway voice. The world teaches the thesis before any mission states it.
- **Crew assembly as verb tutorials:** the outlaw drag strip (Elbows / driving), the layoff livestream (Patch / hacking-and-spoofing), a repossessed satellite church campus (Denny / social infiltration), Canton's hundred acres of handshake deals (Lurlene / the fence economy).
- **Early scores hit deserving local targets:** the HashHoller mine tormenting Hollow Creek's retirees; the Grand Tollway Authority's bill-by-mail fee machine. The crew's brand forms on the Holler feed: they only rob things with a PR department.
- **Act 1 climax — the Fair Job:** a windfall lifted from ParlayPal's "Prediction Pavilion" at the Lone Star State Fair during Texas–OU weekend, the one week the whole Megaplex is in one park. Windfall reprices the economy on schedule (GTA-DNA metronome). That night, an anonymous client called **the Concierge** makes contact: bigger targets, better intel, clean fencing.

### Act 2 — THE CONCIERGE (Oct–Jan) · Midpoint reversal
The Concierge feeds them the villain economy on a platter: a $2M cutting-horse swap during the Droveyards cattle drive, the Harvest Hills water-rights ledger, the Overflow Christmas-pageant tithe vault. The crew gets rich; the montage is glorious; Cass buys back her mother's old midway concession as a front and almost feels done.

- **The pattern the player sees before the crew does:** radio news keeps reporting that *every institution they hit implodes and gets acquired at distress prices — by Bolo Capital.* HouseGoblin values around each target dip, then leap. The satire mechanism is visible in the systems before it's spoken in a cutscene.
- **Midpoint reversal — mission "High Five":** a tipped armored-courier job on LBJ is a task-force trap. The crew scatters across the five levels of the interchange — five escapes, five camera angles, one name — but Denny is taken, and Skye's helpful call summaries become state's evidence. The reveal lands like a gut punch: the Concierge is Bolo's distressed-asset desk. **They weren't robbing the house; they were the house's demolition crew** — an off-books instrument softening targets for acquisition. Cass, the professional, got conned by a term sheet.
- **Low point, deep winter, ice storm:** the metro glazes over; the grid staggers. Bolo announces "GigaStead Campus Catclaw" on the Wynn arena land; HouseGoblin values spike 40%; and the podcast con man who ruined her family rings the Texchange cattle-horn as founder of a "redemption capital" fund. Elbows' granddad's '63 sits in the impound. The crew is scattered, indicted, or drinking. Lurlene, unbothered, starts drawing on the LIVE LAUGH LOVE whiteboard: "Now we do it my way. Slow."

### Act 3 — THE RAIN JOB (Feb–May) · Finale set piece
The long con back, in three moves:

1. **Spring Denny** via the THC-whiplash loophole — the product he was booked over became legal between arrest and arraignment; the writ is a comedy sketch with a court seal, argued by a bus-bench attorney ("CALL 1-800-WRECKED, Se Habla Justice").
2. **Longhorn Dynamics:** the crew stages a fake corporate HQ relocation — renderings, a podcast tour, a leased mirror-glass floor in Las Espejas, one magnificent press conference — and lets three suburbs bid incentives against each other, funding the endgame with the Momentum Council's own subsidy money while planting Cass inside the Texchange listing pipeline as "Longhorn's head of investor relations."
3. **The Rain Job** — the finale worthy of the box art, two boards run in parallel:
   - **Howdy Street:** GigaStead's record-setting IPO on a spring morning with a tornado watch inked across the county line. As the cattle-horn bell rings, Patch's doctored Skye "hallucination" reads GigaStead's true water-and-power numbers aloud to the trading floor in its soothing assistant voice. The crew's short position — held, legally-ish, as ParlayPal "prediction contracts," because betting is illegal in Texas but forecasts aren't — starts printing.
   - **Salvation:** Cass and Elbows breach the flagship compute ranch as the warning drops. Sirens empty the streets (the region's rehearsed reflex, now a stealth mechanic), T-GRID sheds load, ten thousand fans spin down — and for the first time in three years, the town hears silence. The supercell takes the substation on live TV; the storm signs the crew's work.
   - The stock craters, Bolo's leverage implodes, and Tripp Colter learns what "distressed" tastes like from the other side of the table.

**Ending choice** (freedom serving theme): take the payout and vanish — the Ocean's ending — or the **Homestead ending**: dissolve the fortune into land trusts, capped wells, bought-back blocks, and the Catclaw arena reopening under its old name, with Cass's face on nothing. Box art either way: five silhouettes on a High Five ramp, green-black wall cloud behind the Dorado skyline, cash and hail in the air.

## 6. Six Factions

**1. The High Five** *(player crew)*
- **Turf:** migrates with the story — Lurlene's Canton stall → a foreclosed **model home in Harvest Hills™** ("the safest hideout is a staged house nobody has ever lived in") → briefly nowhere at the Act 2 low point.
- **Aesthetic:** pawn-shop Ocean's — pearl snaps, funeral suits, one shared good watch, plan-wall on a builder's whiteboard that still reads LIVE LAUGH LOVE.
- **Role:** protagonist unit; five specialists, one truck; the only faction whose net worth the player watches.

**2. Bolo Capital**
- **Turf:** the Howdy Street tower — and, per the slogan, everywhere: "We Own That Too." Your AC guy, your vet, your landlord, this billboard.
- **Aesthetic:** vests over pearl snaps, term sheets in leather folios, $900 ostrich boots that have never touched dirt; the office smells like a candle called RANCH.
- **Role:** spine villain and the Concierge. Owns the landlord corp, banks the church, underwrites the IPO, fences the crew without their knowledge. Composite frontman: **Tripp Colter III**, who says "distressed" the way other men say "delicious."

**3. GigaStead**
- **Turf:** compute ranches on the Salvation frontier ringed by "ECONOMIC MIRACLE IN PROGRESS" banners; a substation the size of the town it borrowed the name of.
- **Aesthetic:** brushed-steel barns humming at 72 dB beside dry residential wells; a full koi pond out front; private security called **Range Wardens** in tactical ranch-wear.
- **Role:** the Cattle Baron reincarnated as a REIT; the land man knocking on farm doors for "compute." Act 3 target and IPO of the century. Founder composite: **Boone Vandiver**, ex-land man who calls water "a legacy input."

**4. Overflow Fellowship**
- **Turf:** the Shorelake mothership plus 12 video-fed campuses; a parking sea with its own weather.
- **Aesthetic:** fog machines, armed greeters with earpieces, a jet named Blessing One, sermon series marketed like a Marvel phase, tithing app with streak flames.
- **Role:** Act 2's cathedral of the boom — an empire publicly fighting over a disgraced founder's retirement payout while campuses get "sunset." Machine-only satire per binding punch-up rules; Denny is its human cost and its skeleton key.

**5. The Boulevard Clubs**
- **Turf:** Jefferson Blvd / Oak Bluff; MLK on slab Sundays; the impound lot's waiting room, unfortunately.
- **Aesthetic:** candy paint, swangas, club plaques in back windows, airbrushed memorial murals, family picnics at the hop-off.
- **Role:** allies with an honor code (no guns on club jobs; slow earns respect). Elbows' world, the neighborhoods the boom is eating, and the story's moral counterweight — when the crew must choose an ending, the Clubs are the faces of the Homestead option.

**6. The Momentum Council**
- **Turf:** the Brisco–Blando–Salvation ribbon-cutting circuit; hotel ballrooms; a podcast stage inside a former Cheesecake Factory.
- **Aesthetic:** golf polos, giant novelty scissors, renderings of buildings that will never exist, lanyards all the way down.
- **Role:** the relocation-subsidy grift personified — mayors, "economic momentum officers," and Pioneer Vision Development in one mutual-appreciation machine. Act 3 mark: the Longhorn Dynamics con lets the crew rob three cities with a press release.

*(The Grand Tollway Authority and the Street-Racing Task Force operate as the ambient law/heat layer rather than a story faction — the GTA's cameras and fee-collectors are the wanted system wearing a lanyard, and every star of heat arrives with an itemized invoice.)*

## 7. Ten Signature Missions

1. **Grudge Night** — win a cash grudge race at the outlaw eighth-mile to recruit Elbows, then lose the task force using frontage roads and Texas-turnaround 180s — a chase grammar no other city has. **[DFW-only]**
2. **The Hum** — steal HashHoller's immersion-cooling manifolds so Hollow Creek sleeps one night; the PR guy's decibel meter finally reads zero, and the retirees with clipboards toast you at dawn.
3. **Fair and Square** — lift ParlayPal's prediction-vault take at the Lone Star State Fair during Texas–OU weekend; exfil through the midway in a corny-dog supply truck while Tall Tom's "HOWDY, FOLKS" covers the alarms. **[DFW-only]**
4. **The Toll** — hijack the Grand Tollway Authority's bill-by-mail data van on the Tollway at night and mail 400,000 refunds; the getaway is scored by every gantry you blow through flashing you a fine. **[DFW-only]**
5. **The Herd** — swap a $2M cutting-horse stallion out of a Futurity gala using the Droveyards' twice-daily longhorn drive as moving cover; the getaway proceeds at cattle speed. **[DFW-only]**
6. **Silent Night, Holy Smoke** — empty Overflow's tithe vault mid-Christmas-pageant (flying angels, 1,000-voice choir) and escape northbound on the live camels, up the Tollway, against traffic.
7. **High Five** — the midpoint betrayal: a five-level scatter across the interchange, each crew member escaping on a different ramp — sniper drone above, task force below, ribbon-pasta geometry in between. **[DFW-only]**
8. **Model Home** — infiltrate Harvest Hills' Founders Day as a relocating couple; crack the sales office for the ledger proving Pioneer Vision sold the water rights before it sold the homes.
9. **Longhorn Dynamics** — stage an entire fake HQ relocation — renderings, podcast tour, one leased floor in Las Espejas' mirror towers — and walk out with three cities' incentive wires at the ribbon-cutting.
10. **The Rain Job** — finale: short GigaStead at the Texchange cattle-horn bell, then breach the Salvation compute ranch inside a tornado warning as sirens empty the streets and T-GRID sheds load — the weather is the crew's inside man. **[DFW-only]**

## 8. Five Satire Set Pieces (2026 targets, receipts in canon)

1. **The Grid Resilience Gala** *(ERCOT / data-center demand doubling; 49B gallons of water in 2026)* — GigaStead hosts a black-tie gala celebrating grid reliability during a rolling Conservation Appeal; generators power the ice sculptures, valet EVs brick in the dark, and the crew works the room by candlelight while the radio reports "the grid performed as designed."
2. **The Keynote** *(AI hype-cycle; AGI "18 months away," quarterly, since 2023)* — at OmniMind's Brisco developer day, Patch swaps the live demo so Skye reads the executives' internal messages aloud in its soothing assistant voice; the crowd applauds, assuming it's the product. Playable satire in the Lifeinvader-mission lineage.
3. **The Cattle-Horn Bell** *(Texas Stock Exchange live, July 2026)* — Howdy Street IPO theater: podcast row, VCs in unmuddied boots saying "Texas is the new Rome," a redemption-fund founder ringing the horn. The finale's trading-floor half plays out inside this set.
4. **The Sign Flip** *(THC gray-market whiplash; an $8B industry built on a farm-bill typo)* — mid-heist at a Smoke 'Em If You Got 'Em vape barn, the law changes AGAIN: the marquee flips NOW LEGAL → NEVER MIND → ASK BUBBA while the crew is inside, and the contraband in their truck becomes legal product between the theft and the roadblock. Charges evaporate; the mechanic is the joke.
5. **The Pageant** *(megachurch industrial complex; the payout war)* — Overflow's "Gift of Glory": movie-trailer sermon marketing, wire-flown angels, live camels, and a tithing-leaderboard jumbotron ("the Book of Numbers") counting UP while the vault beneath it counts down. Institution-only satire; the congregation is filmed with warmth.

## 9. Tone and Radio Identity

**Tone:** *Ocean's Eleven* × *Hell or High Water* × *The Big Short*, with *King of the Hill*'s affection for its people. Elmore Leonard dialogue — nobody explains the joke, courtesy curdles into menace, and the funniest line in every scene is the invoice. Texans are in on the joke, never the butt of it (binding canon rule): HollerBurger is beloved AND absurd; the villain is never a person with a drawl, it's a machine with a mission statement.

**Radio:** the nine-station dial from satire-targets.md ships as-is; this pitch adds the heist layer.

- **No non-diegetic score.** Every job is soundtracked by whatever the getaway vehicle's radio is playing, so crews argue over the dial as characterization: Elbows locks Grind 105.7 (the "Toll Road Freestyle" traffic report doubles as chase intel); Lurlene keeps Patriot Talk on "to know what the marks are scared of this week"; Denny cannot make himself turn off KSOL's Seed-Faith Power Half-Hour; Patch mutes everything and the silence is its own bit.
- **La Jefa 104.5** — canonically the only accurate traffic report on the dial — is a genuine gameplay asset during escapes; learning to trust it over the map UI is a player rite of passage.
- **The Warpath 1310's "DAYS SINCE LAST CHAMPIONSHIP" counter** ticks all year like a metronome of civic delusion; the Rustlers' season quietly tracks the crew's arc (playoff collapse lands the same week as the midpoint betrayal).
- **99.9 The Singularity** (GigaStead-owned, AI DJ "Skye") occasionally addresses Cass by her billing name mid-getaway — ambient dread that pays off when Skye becomes state's evidence, then the finale's murder weapon.
- **News as scoreboard:** Megaplex Public Radio and the Holler feed report every heist in real time, mispricing the details; hearing your own job described wrong is the reward loop.

## 10. Open-World Expression

Between missions the player *runs cons and runs from the consequences*, and every freedom loop argues the thesis (growth is God; everything is a costume):

- **The grift economy (repeatable verbs):** midway hustles at fair time, hail-season counter-scams (con the roofing scammers), HOA-fine liberation jobs (rescue the flamingo), toll-refund runs, open-house casing in wine-mom cosplay. Small cons feed the authenticity wardrobe and crew trust.
- **Driving cultures as reputation systems (canon car bible):** slab Sundays where *slow* earns respect, takeover nights with an escalating task-force ladder, grudge cash at the outlaw strip, truck meets migrating one exit down the frontage road when police push.
- **Markets as toys — playable satire:** HouseGoblin home values and ParlayPal "forecast" prices react to your heists and the news cycle they generate; the informed player trades on their own chaos, Lester-style. The endgame mechanic (shorting the boom) is practiced here all game.
- **The property ladder as the anti-GTA endgame sink:** money buys back blocks — Oak Bluff storefronts, Tenth-Street-style shotgun houses, the Catclaw arena. Each rescued deed visibly changes the district (the "coming soon" signs come down), building the world-memory GTA never had and pre-loading the Homestead ending.
- **Storm season:** chase supercells alongside the Storm Warlord's broadcast crews for loot-window intel; hail dents every car in the sim and spawns a body-shop economy; sirens empty streets — a systemic stealth window any player can learn to exploit, not just the finale.
- **Crew hangouts (trust = unlocks):** karaoke in the Carrollton K-town, bull-riding at the mega-honky-tonk, coffee-ceremony sit-downs with neighborhood elders, First Monday fencing trips. Hangouts raise crew competence the way GTA 5's cheap crew members visibly failed.
- **Seasonal world events:** the Fair's 24 days, December pageants and playoff caravans, January Stock Show, Tết and Diwali festivals — the calendar keeps free-roam fresh and keeps the satire ambient.

## 11. The Only-in-DFW Test

- **The villain economy is literally headquartered here in 2026.** The biggest single-family PE landlord: HQ'd in Dallas. No. 1 HQ-relocation metro in America. The Texas Stock Exchange: opened on real Y'all Street in July 2026. Megachurch capital of the continent. The data-center land rush and its seceded grid: Texas-only phenomena. Los Santos satirizes fame; Vice City satirizes vice; only the Megaplex can satirize the **boomtown gospel** — growth as God — because the boom is *here*, now, with receipts.
- **White-collar crime is the regional heritage.** Dallas was the world capital of the S&L collapse; the I-30 land-flip scandal is a heist blueprint with a local ZIP code; today's affinity-fraud and crypto-Ponzi cases are in DFW courtrooms. A con-artist protagonist isn't imported genre here — she's a native species. (And Bonnie and Clyde were West Dallas kids; the outlaw-crew DNA is buried in local cemeteries.)
- **Geometry no other map has:** a dumbbell metro with two full downtowns; frontage-road/Texas-turnaround chase grammar; the five-level High Five as a set piece with its own name; a fairground with a 55-foot talking cowboy; longhorn cattle drives as scheduled traffic events; a mile-wide empty floodway under a white arch; a tollway surveillance state that bills you for existing.
- **The sky is a mechanic.** Supercells, gorilla hail, siren culture, Conservation Appeals, ice-storm shutdowns — DFW weather empties streets, kills lights, and reprices assets *on a schedule a heist crew can plan around*. No coastal map can make the atmosphere a crew member.
- **The class map is drivable.** The Tollway is a literal money gradient from Uptown glass to Salvation slab-farms; the 5 a.m. servant-economy migration and 6 p.m. reverse flow tell the story in traffic. The satire is legible at 80 mph, before a single line of dialogue.

## 12. Risks and Rating Notes

- **Rating: M.** Violence (restrained; guns-as-failure-state lowers body count vs. genre norm), strong language, gambling themes (the APP is always the villain — no glamorization; ParlayPal's 2 a.m. push notifications are horror, not aspiration), drug references (THC-whiplash satire), mild sexual content.
- **Church satire is the highest-wire act.** Binding punch-up rules apply verbatim: the abuse behind the real 2024–26 scandal is NEVER material; targets are the payout fight, the PR machine, the jet, the seed-faith economics. Congregants and faith itself are portrayed warmly (Denny's arc is about losing an institution, not losing God). Any pageant-heist beat that reads as mocking worshippers gets cut.
- **Composite-character discipline.** Boone Vandiver, Tripp Colter (Bolo), Pastor Chad Everly, Colt Bidwell: each must draw from ≥3 real-world sources plus invention, with no one-to-one biography mapping — especially Vandiver (avoid mapping to any single real data-center or ex-governor figure) and the redemption-podcast con man (fully fictional case).
- **The "Grand Tollway Authority (GTA)" acronym gag** needs legal review against Take-Two trademark sensitivity; it's a background sign joke, cheap to rename (e.g., "Lone Star Tollway Collective") if counsel blinks.
- **Tornado content vs. real trauma.** The finale storm is fictional, tracks no real event (no 2019 EF3 path recreation), and sirens are treated with the respect locals give them; disaster harm to bystanders happens off-screen. Hail is comedy; casualties are not.
- **Comedy blunting stakes** — the classic heist-comedy failure. Mitigation is structural: fail-forward consequences (a blown job spawns heat, arrests, and worse fallback plans instead of a checkpoint reset), Denny's Act 2 arrest is permanent story, and the Boulevard Clubs' losses keep the boom's cost human.
- **Solo-dev scope honesty.** Five voiced companions is the biggest cost line. Mitigations per GTA-DNA doc: one playable lead (no switching tech), crew as systemic layer with barks over cutscenes, staggered recruitment across Act 1, missions defined as simulation goals ("empty the vault") not scripts, and the seasonal calendar reusing world events the open world needs anyway. The heist genre's prep-fatigue risk is met by making prep missions open-world verbs whose payoff is visible in execution (canon §2 prep→execute rule).
- **Satire drift.** Every ad, station, and side quest must cite the one-line thesis — *growth is God, and everything is a costume* — or it doesn't ship. Scattershot is GTA 5's documented weakness; this pitch's discipline is its edge.
