# Story Direction — v1 (July 2026)

**Status: RATIFIED by Milad, 2026-07-29. Title: EL DORADO GRANDE.**
This document is the story bible. The Story Director owns it; changes go through them and get logged in `docs/decisions.md`.

## The verdict

Three judges (narrative craft, open-world game design, Texas cultural authenticity) independently ranked the three pitches identically:

1. **Pitch A — the character saga** (`pitch-a-character-saga.md`) — WINNER
2. Pitch B — satire-forward heist comedy (`pitch-b-satire-heist.md`)
3. Pitch C — multi-protagonist ensemble (`pitch-c-ensemble.md`)

Why A won: it's the only pitch where protagonist psychology, signature mechanic, and theme are a single object. Booker "Book" Reyes is a bull rider whose discipline is holding on and whose need is learning what to let go of; his special ability literalizes his trauma; the midpoint makes him *complicit* (he helped clear his own neighborhood), not merely betrayed; and he has the rarest asset in open-world crime — a home worth losing. Its premise generates *systems, not scripts*: the Hook (repo board), the Paper (title fraud), the Slow Lane (slab respect), the Saddle (horse layer), storm season as an economy. B's flaws are load-bearing (hollow protagonist, social-sim dependency); C's are structural and violate the solo-dev canon ("never three"). A's flaws are fixable — by grafting.

## The spine (from Pitch A — read it in full)

- **Protagonist:** Booker "Book" Reyes, 34 — ex-bull-rider repo man, Black trail-ride family on one side, Cedar Cliff charro family on the other. Four verbs: Hook, Saddle, Slow Lane, Paper. Special: "The Full Eight" (8-second time dilation).
- **Logline:** A broke ex-bull-rider repossesses cars across a booming Texas metroplex — until he discovers the land flips funding his family's survival are erasing his own neighborhood, and turns his tow hook on the boom itself.
- **Structure:** one calendar year; the DFW weather calendar IS the act structure (supercell spring → furnace summer → Fair season fall → ice-storm coda).
- **Midpoint:** Book traces his biggest flip to Gar Voss / GigaStead's "Project Alamo" — the operation he works for was *founded* to use the Bottoms' own son to clear the Bottoms.
- **Finale:** vault heist at the Lone Star State Fair as an out-of-season tornado evacuates the midway; horseback escape past a burning Tall Tom; confrontation at the flooding data-center campus.
- **Three endings:** Take the Money / Burn the Paper / The Full Eight (turn everyone in, himself included).
- **Theme:** extraction vs. stewardship. The neighborhood is never the joke — the paperwork is.

## Binding amendments (judge-mandated grafts)

### Title
**EL DORADO GRANDE** — ratified by Milad, 2026-07-29. ("COMIN' DOWN" was retired as a Houston-slang authenticity foul; ALL HAT and THE FULL EIGHT were the panel candidates.) The title reads as the world's own voice: the city is Dorado, and El Dorado is a golden city that was never real — the boom's promise in three words.

### From Pitch C
- **Wade "The Bullet" Boone joins as a major NPC** (C's own fallback design): the leveraged Friday-night-football legend — dealership pitchman, 96-month truck notes, ParlayPal ledger. Book repossesses his truck in Act 1; he becomes the tragicomic fixer for the Paper missions and the satire face of suburban credit. His **"one true thing" videoboard confession** becomes a mission climax. He also *frames the loud missions*, giving mayhem-inclined players a pressure valve A lacked.
- **The two-meter world ledger:** **Respect** (earned in communities) vs. **Goblin Value** (assets, repriced hourly by the HouseGoblin app). Mechanizes extraction-vs-stewardship and gates which endings are offered.
- **Per-faction ambient response layers** — HOA drones, Bolo process servers, GigaStead "Range Wardens," Overflow ear-pieced greeters who arrive BEFORE police. Where you commit a crime changes who comes. (Judges: best open-world system idea of the discovery phase.)
- **The bark canon:** five-topic overheard dialogue — "Where'd you go to high school?", "This is our year" (dead eyes), Dr Zing, delays measured in Pecos Bullets, weather talk all year.
- **The naming set** (to naming bible, reconcile conflicts below): the Big Howdy, the Wishbone, the Neon Mustang, Rendezvous Tower, Los Espejos, Midfield International, Dr Zing, Muscadine, the Green Light.
- **Playable ice storm:** upgrade the epilogue ice storm to a playable coda (ice physics flip the driving model; generator glow marks the rich streets).
- **Faction supply-chain org chart:** GigaStead needs land+water ← Bolo supplies squeezed blocks ← Overflow supplies the blessing ← Drover supplies the myth. Attacking one link visibly stresses the others.
- **"Own That Too" ending logic:** darken "Take the Money" so it explicitly makes Book the new machine.

### From Pitch B
- **Reveal planting:** surface the Voss/GigaStead pattern in systems before any cutscene states it — radio news reports every institution Book hits getting acquired at distress prices; HouseGoblin values dip-then-leap around his targets. (Fixes the midpoint-contrivance risk.)
- **"Longhorn Dynamics"** — the fake-HQ-relocation con (renderings, a podcast tour, one leased mirror-glass floor; three suburbs bid incentive packages against each other) as the peak Act 2 Paper heist.
- **Crew trust meters** for Squall, Sol, Sudden: low-trust companions fail loudly mid-heist; max-trust ones improvise unscripted saves. Systematizes "each closing costs him someone."
- **The authenticity/wardrobe stat:** boot wear, hat crease, truck-bed scratches gate infiltrations — mechanizes Book's "same man, just hats" identity.
- **Diegetic-only mission scoring:** jobs are soundtracked by the getaway vehicle's radio; **La Jefa 104.5's traffic reports are accurate** and learnable escape intel; The Warpath runs a "DAYS SINCE LAST CHAMPIONSHIP" counter; 99.9 The Singularity's AI DJ addresses the player by billing name mid-getaway.
- **The "Sign Flip"** THC mechanic: legality flips *mid-mission* — contraband becomes product between theft and roadblock.
- **ParlayPal "prediction contracts"** as the legal-gray market toy, culminating in a playable short against GigaStead's Texchange IPO inside the "Take the Money" ending.
- **Goodwill jobs** (the neighborhood wins mid-game, not just at the end): **"The Toll"** — hijack the Grand Tollway Authority's bill-by-mail van and mail 400,000 refunds; **"The Hum"** — steal the crypto-mine's cooling manifolds so the Granbury-inspired retirees can sleep.

## Known weaknesses to manage (judge flags)
1. **Satire density** — A deliberately backgrounds satire ("the water, not the point"), which drifts from GTA identity. The grafts (Wade, Longhorn Dynamics, goodwill jobs, radio systems) are the fix; Head Writer owns keeping the dial and billboards HOT.
2. **Gar Voss is thin** — needs a real characterization pass; the reveal-planting graft helps but the man himself needs interiority.
3. **Mayhem dissonance** — a sincere protagonist with a home makes rampage play dissonant; Wade-framed loud missions + the conduct-reading world (Respect meter, district memory) must carry it.
4. **Cultural authenticity load** — Black trail-ride, charro, slab, and lowrider cultures need paid community consultants and community voice casting before content ships. This is the soul of the game and its highest execution risk.
5. **Scope ladder** (from A §12, binding): seasons may become act-scripted weather states; horse layer may confine to the Bottoms + finale; hail damage may limit to player vehicle + scripted lots. **The Hook and the Paper are non-negotiable — they ARE the game.**
