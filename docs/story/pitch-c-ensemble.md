# Pitch C — "ALL HAT" (GTA5-Style Multi-Protagonist Ensemble)

**Lens:** Three playable protagonists whose arcs interlock across the metroplex's three worlds — Dorado glam/finance, Fort Verde cowtown grit, and the northern boom corridor. Switching is the narrative device: the same land grab seen from opposite sides of the class divide. Their collision course is the plot.
**Canon grounding:** dfw-geography.md, dfw-culture.md, satire-targets.md, gta-design-dna.md. Thesis (per design DNA §5): *the boomtown gospel — everything is growth, and growth is God.*

---

## 1. Working Title + Alternates

- **Working title: ALL HAT** — from the Fort Worth insult for Dallas: "all hat, no cattle." Two words that contain the entire thesis: performed wealth, borrowed authenticity, a metroplex cosplaying its own myth. The title is also the final scorecard for every faction in the game.
- **Alternate 1: THE BIG HOWDY** — our parody name for the High Five interchange (five levels of ramps where you wave at people you'll never meet). Warmth with teeth; the interchange is the box art.
- **Alternate 2: BOOMTOWN GOSPEL** — the thesis as a title; best if marketing wants the satire on the tin.

**Parody naming (adopting satire-canon provisional set, proposing the unsettled):**
Canon-adopted: Dallas → **Dorado**, Fort Worth → **Fort Verde**, the Metroplex → **the Megaplex**, Frisco → **Brisco**, Plano → **Blando**, Arlington → **Middlington**, Celina → **Salvation**, Southlake → **Shorelake**, Granbury → **Hollow Creek**, AT&T Stadium → **ClingTel Colosseum ("the Ego Dome")**, State Fair/Big Tex → **Lone Star State Fair / Tall Tom**, Texas Stock Exchange → **The Texchange** on **Howdy Street**.
Newly proposed here: High Five → **The Big Howdy** · Trinity River → **Divinity River** ("the Div"; Great Trinity Forest → **the Div Bottoms**) · Oak Cliff → **The Bluff** (Jefferson Blvd → **Obregón Boulevard**) · Deep Ellum → **Deep Ash** (downtown streets named for trees; Elm warps to Ash) · Fair Park/Cotton Bowl → **Jubilee Park / The Boll** · DFW Airport → **Midfield International** · Las Colinas → **Los Espejos** ("the Mirrors") · Grapevine → **Muscadine** · Stockyards → **The Chisholm Yards** (Billy Bob's → **Big Bud's Texas**) · The Star → **The Brand, Brisco** (cattle brand = marketing brand) · Dallas North Tollway → **Golden Spur Tollway** (run by canon's Grand Tollway Authority) · Reunion Tower → **Rendezvous Tower** · Bank of America Plaza → **Cattleman's Trust Tower ("the Green Light")** · Margaret Hunt Hill Bridge → **The Wishbone** · Pegasus sign → **the Neon Mustang**.

## 2. Logline

A repo driver from the Bluff, a broke Friday-night-football legend, and an aging Black cowboy discover they're three corners of the same AI-age land grab — and drive, con, and ride against the boomtown gospel that owns the Megaplex.

---

## 3. Era and Season

**Present day: one full year, August 2026 → May 2027.** Two reasons, both load-bearing:

1. **The satire is perishable and peaking.** The Texchange went live July 2026. The AI data-center land rush, the grid liturgy, Y'all Street, transit secession votes, the $100K pickup — all of it is happening *right now* and won't keep. Period pieces embalm satire; this one has to be wet paint.
2. **In DFW, weather is the act structure.** Canon says "sky is terrain here" — so the seasons are the three acts: **Act 1 = the heat dome** (August furnace, Conservation Appeals, brown lawns, heat shimmer as menace). **Midpoint = the Lone Star State Fair** (late Sept–Oct, the whole Megaplex in one park). **Act 2's low point = the ice storm** (January: 4,000 overpasses become skating rinks, the grid fails, generator-glow marks the rich neighborhoods). **Act 3 = supercell season** (April–May: green-black skies, gorilla hail, and one tornado saved for the finale). No other American city hands you a dramatic arc in its forecast.

**Opening-week texture:** the world's-biggest-soccer-tournament-alike has just left town — deflating "WELCOME, WORLD" banners on the Ego Dome, invoices coming due, a civic hangover that primes the pump for the next miracle (Project Longhorn) to be announced. Boomtowns always need the *next* boom; the story starts in the trough between two of them.

## 4. The Protagonists

Three lenses, three generations (26 / 47 / 58), three geographies. Each has a home faction, a home radio station, and a special ability. Per design-DNA §3, each switch-in lands on a hand-authored life vignette.

### MARISOL "MARI" REYNA — 26 — The Bluff, South Dorado
- **Background:** Daughter of a lowrider garage on Obregón Boulevard; her late father's Coronado Car Club plaque still hangs in the back window of his '84. Days: tow-truck and valet shifts across the class line in Uptown. Nights: spotter, then driver, in the takeover scene. Bolo Capital hires her crew to run repos — until she finds her own block on the acquisition list.
- **Want:** Buy back the garage and the block before Bolo's "revitalization" erases it. **Need:** To learn that owning a piece of the machine is not the same as beating it — the ledger doesn't care whose name is on it.
- **Playstyle identity:** The wheel. Best driver on the roster; slab/lowrider customization is her progression tree; "slow is the point" cruising builds respect while speed spends it. Special: **Slipstream** — focus-time weaving through traffic.
- **Why players love her:** She crosses every line on the map daily — valet stand at the Green Light tower by night, the Bluff by dawn. She is the player's own class tourism made honest, and the funniest side-eye in the game.
- **Switch-in vignettes:** mid-hop-off argument at a Coronado picnic; asleep in the tow truck outside a Howdy Street gala; teaching her cousin to parallel-park the '84; being written up by a valet manager half as smart as her.
- **Orbit:** **Güero** (cousin, garage bookkeeper, catastrophically honest); **Tía Lupe** (Obregón dress-shop owner, neighborhood intelligence network); **"Trill Association"** (Grind 105.7 DJ who narrates her takeover legend without knowing her name).

### WADE "THE BULLET" BOONE — 47 — Brisco
- **Background:** Threw for 400 yards in the '97 state final at the Boll; peaked at 17 and the town never let him land. Now: car-dealership pitchman ("Boone Trucks — Appreciate You!"), Stonebridle Ranch HOA president, Overflow Fellowship usher, and secretly leveraged to death — 96-month Steed Longrider loans, a HashHoller "mining allocation," and a ParlayPal ledger he checks at funerals. The Rustlers organization rents his myth for the Project Longhorn IPO roadshow.
- **Want:** Keep the facade until his jersey retirement ceremony at the Ego Dome. **Need:** To stop being the town's myth and tell one true thing in public — even if it detonates his life.
- **Playstyle identity:** The pressure valve. Guns, heavies, and spectacle; violence delivered with "sir" and "ma'am" — courtesy curdling into menace, per culture canon. Special: **Pocket Presence** — the old QB reads the field; bullet-time aim plus damage soak.
- **Why players love him:** He's Michael and Trevor fused in one golf polo — public dignity, private demolition — and the town keeps forgiving him because he won State. The comedy engine and the tragedy in one body.
- **Switch-in vignettes:** driving range at 2 a.m. in a full suit; chairing an HOA hearing about a flamingo lawn ornament with barely contained violence; filming take 14 of a dealership ad; checking ParlayPal at his mother-in-law's birthday dinner.
- **Orbit:** **Deb Boone** (wife, Shorelake-realtor-in-training, sees through him and stays anyway); **Coach Yancy** (his old coach, now a Rustlers "culture consultant," the man Wade can't disappoint); **Randy "the Hoss Whisperer" Klum** (Warpath 1310 host who re-litigates Wade's '97 final weekly).

### OTIS "DUB" FREEMAN — 58 — Northside Fort Verde / the western scrub
- **Background:** Third-generation horseman in the Bill Pickett lineage (1 in 4 cowboys was Black; Dub makes sure you know it). Rides drag in the Chisholm Yards' daily longhorn drive for tourist tips; president of the **Freedman's Ride** trail club; runs a horse lot whose grazing lease sits on the aquifer GigaStead is quietly drilling. The land men keep knocking. He keeps not selling.
- **Want:** Hold the land and keep the Ride's Sunday processions alive. **Need:** To accept that the tradition survives by changing hands and changing form — and to let the young ones (Mari's generation) carry it.
- **Playstyle identity:** The tactician. Horseback traversal as a first-class vehicle lane (Trinity Trails, the Div Bottoms, freeway shoulders); long guns, rope/lasso verbs, stealth rustling, animal handling. Special: **Steady Hand** — heartbeat-slow rifle focus and a calm that settles horses and rooms.
- **Why players love him:** The oldest playable lead in the genre, and the only sane man on the map. Riding a horse under a five-level interchange while the radio argues about compute ranches is the whole game in one image.
- **Switch-in vignettes:** mucking stalls at blue dawn with KRID low; dominoes at the feed store with three men who remember everything; chasing hogs out of the Div Bottoms camp; posing stone-faced for a tourist's selfie mid-cattle-drive.
- **Orbit:** **Miss Earlene** (Ride matriarch, Juneteenth committee, the only person Dub obeys); **Junior** (his estranged son, an Alliance-corridor logistics manager who thinks the horses are a museum); **Reyna Sr.** (Mari's late father — Dub knew him from Sunday processions past the garage; the ghost that ties the trio's worlds together).

**How the triangle meets (world tissue, pre-plot):** the Freedman's Ride's Sunday route crosses Obregón Boulevard past the Coronado garage; Wade's dealership sponsors the Cowtown Colosseum rodeo Dub works; Mari's valet stand serves the tower where Bolo signs the deal. The map makes the collisions before the script does.

**Ensemble logic (why three, stated plainly):** The Megaplex is a dumbbell with a boom corridor — a polycentric metro (geography canon §1). One lens flattens it into one city; two gives you a rivalry; three gives you the *triangle trade* — southside labor, suburban credit, and rural land, all feeding the same gospel. Switching is how the player audits the machine from all three books at once.

**Switching design (the mechanic, specified):**
- **Free-roam switching** from the character wheel at any time outside missions; every switch-in lands on a vignette (above) — the GTA 5 trick with the highest characterization ROI per dollar.
- **Mid-mission prongs** in the act-climax scores only (Big Howdy, Fair Warning, Conservation Appeal, Groundbreaking): hard-authored handoffs, sniper–wheel–saddle geometry, used sparingly so each one is an event.
- **Rashomon replays** (this pitch's novelty): after key story missions, replay the same level as a different lead who was *there* — the repo that opens Mari's game is a humiliation cutscene in Wade's; the Fair triple-cross is three full playthroughs of one night. One level built, two or three stories told: an ensemble feature priced for a solo dev.
- **Cross-lead consequences:** heat, injuries, and reputation persist per character; ditch a chase as Mari and swap to Dub, and the radio still hunts her — switching is never an eraser, only a lens change.

## 5. Three-Act Arc

**The machine: PROJECT LONGHORN.** GigaStead's bid to build the largest compute ranch on Earth in the Megaplex: land assembled by Pioneer Vision Development on the northern frontier, "workforce housing" and Divinity River water rights squeezed out of the Bluff by Bolo Capital, moral cover preached by Overflow Fellowship, hype supplied by the Rustlers, and the whole thing financed by a Texchange IPO rung in with a cattle-horn bell. Every protagonist is hired by it before they ever learn its name. The fixer who hires all three: **Hoyt Kessler, "the Big Friendly"** — an economic-development consultant (canon's Relocation Booster archetype) who has never once said the word "traffic."

### ACT 1 — HEAT DOME (August–September)
Three separate lives, three money problems, one shimmer of menace over everything. Mari runs Bolo repos and discovers the Bluff on the acquisition list; Wade signs as Longhorn's "hometown face" while collecting ParlayPal debts on the side; Dub's lease is cancelled the same week GigaStead's test wells go in and his neighbors' taps sputter.
- **Beats:** each lead's opening triptych doubles as a diegetic tutorial (Mari: driving and heat evasion; Wade: gunplay and social access; Dub: horse, rope, stealth) per design-DNA §6; a Conservation Appeal night establishes the grid liturgy; Mari meets Wade by repossessing *his* truck out of *his* dealership — enemies at 6 a.m., partners by Act 2.
- **Climax and first windfall:** the accidental team-up on **the Big Howdy armored-car job** reprices all three lives at once — and Dub, hired separately to move the cargo west by wagon trail because no one watches a horse, recognizes the money's return address. First act-break windfall lands on the economy metronome (design-DNA §6).

### ACT 2 — FAIR TO FREEZE (October–February)
**MIDPOINT REVERSAL — "Fair Warning," at the Lone Star State Fair.** All three work separate jobs at the Fair on Texas–Oklahoma weekend: Mari lifts a courier bag under the Boll's roar, Wade shakes down a booster in the livestock barns, Dub moves a stolen stallion through the trailer lots. The three jobs converge at Tall Tom's boots — same drop, same night — and the paperwork in the bag proves the Big Friendly hired all three *against each other*, each one built to be another's fall guy. The reversal: the enemies realize they're colleagues; the crew forms over corny dogs while the Fair's fireworks cover the sirens.
Back half, on the prep→execute loop (design-DNA §2): united prep-heists — the whistleblower accountant out of Los Espejos; the SeedFaith ledger out of Overflow's arena campus during the Christmas pageant; a Stock Show week of livestock-auction laundering surveillance at Dickies-alike arena. Between scores, low-stakes character missions reset tension: Wade coaches Güero's flag-football team into a fistfight; Mari drives Miss Earlene's Juneteenth committee; Dub teaches Wade to sit a horse (Wade is terrible; the town films it).
**Low point, the ice storm:** the machine strikes back preemptively — Bolo forecloses the Coronado garage, Dub's stock is impounded on a rustling frame, and Wade is publicly burned as the IPO's "rogue bad actor," his jersey ceremony cancelled on live TV. Fail-forward, not reset (design-DNA §7): every loss becomes Act 3 inventory — the impound lot, the foreclosure paperwork, and Wade's disgrace are all exploited in the counter-flip. The trio raids GigaStead's private substation the night T-GRID sheds load on everyone else, and steals the thing the deal cannot replace: its own books.

### ACT 3 — SUPERCELL (April–May)
The counter-flip: run the 1980s S&L playbook *at* the machine — straw buyers, appraisal fraud in reverse, a short position built through the in-game market (playable satire per design-DNA §5). The act runs on the S&L playbook as gameplay: recruit straw buyers from every community the machine burned, flip parcels in a chain before lunch, and walk the Big Friendly into certifying fraudulent appraisals on camera. Wade's disgrace becomes the weapon — nobody fact-checks a fallen legend who wants back in.
**FINALE SET PIECE — "The Groundbreaking," the box-art mission:** IPO listing day doubles as Project Longhorn's groundbreaking as a supercell walls up in the west. Three simultaneous, fully switchable prongs: **Dub** leads the Freedman's Ride — three hundred riders and a borrowed longhorn herd — up the Golden Spur Tollway to blockade the site (cattle drive as traffic event: only here); **Mari** runs the escrow convoy through gorilla hail with the task force in the mirrors; **Wade**, at the Ego Dome celebration rally, hijacks the world's largest video board and plays the stolen ledger to eighty thousand people as his one true thing. Sirens. The funnel drops — on Harvest Hills' empty model homes, the only victimless address in the Megaplex. Radio, next morning: "the market performed as designed."
**Box art:** Dub on horseback on the Big Howdy's top ramp, longhorns flooding the levels below; Mari's candy-paint slab mid-swang, doors up; Wade on the hood of a hail-dented Steed Longrider; behind them, both skylines and the wall cloud, lit green.
**Final choice (three endings, choose your thesis):** *Take the Money* (the short pays; leave the Megaplex richer than the myth), *Burn the Book* (collapse the deal publicly; keep the block, stay poor, stay home), or *Own That Too* (the trio becomes the new machine — growth wins, because it always does).

## 6. Six Factions

| Faction | Turf | Aesthetic | Narrative role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **GigaStead** | Compute ranches on the western scrub and northern frontier; a glass "Neighborhood Relations Center" in Blando | Windowless gray barns at 72 dB, koi ponds behind razor wire, "ECONOMIC MIRACLE IN PROGRESS" banners, land men in Patagonia vests and unstained Stetsons | The cattle baron reincarnated as a REIT; prime antagonist — its wells, substations, and PR are the mission targets |
| **Bolo Capital** | Everywhere, via ownership: Howdy Street tower, build-to-rent seas, squeezed blocks of the Bluff | No aesthetic — that's the joke; beige LLCs, same hold music at your vet, your AC guy, your landlord | The invisible villain; Mari's employer-then-target; "We Own That Too" made playable through repo and eviction missions |
| **Overflow Fellowship** | Twelve campuses; arena flagship in Blando; SeedFaith app in every pocket | Fog-machine worship, movie-trailer sermon series ("Blessed & Zoned"), armed greeters, jet named Blessing One | Moral cover for the gospel of growth; launders the deal's virtue; Wade's church and Act 2 infiltration ground |
| **The Dorado Rustlers Organization** | The Ego Dome, Middlington; The Brand, Brisco | "America's Franchise™": owner-for-life Colt Bidwell, 84, still calling plays; sponsorship decals on the practice chapel | Celebrity-industrial complex; rents Wade's myth to the IPO; comic royalty whose stake in Longhorn is the Act 3 pressure point |
| **The Coronado Car Club** | Obregón Boulevard, the Bluff; Slab Sundays past Jubilee Park | Candy paint, swangas, airbrushed Virgens and lost fathers, plaques in back windows, family picnics at shows | Mari's home and honor code; the neighborhood the whole plot is trying to buy; hop-offs, cruises, and the game's warmest rooms |
| **The Freedman's Ride** | Northside Fort Verde stables, Trinity Trails, the Div Bottoms, Sunday processions through the Chisholm Yards | Trail-ride flags, zydeco and southern soul, mud-caked Ariats (the boots that pass the authenticity check) | The moral center and living heritage; Dub's family in all but blood; the finale's cavalry — treated with full dignity per canon |

**Faction dynamics (the machine's org chart, the allies' seams):**
- The three corporate factions form a supply chain — GigaStead needs land and water, Bolo supplies the squeezed blocks, Overflow supplies the blessing — and the Rustlers supply the myth. Attacking any link stresses the others; the Act 3 counter-flip works because the player has learned the chain by working for every link.
- The three home factions barely know each other in Act 1 — the Ride thinks the car clubs are noise, the car clubs think the boosters are marks, the boosters think both are scenery. The trio's alliance forces the alliances; the finale's tollway blockade is all three cultures in one frame.
- **Systemic texture:** each faction runs its own ambient patrol/response layer — HOA drones and tow trucks in Pioneer Vision's subdivisions, Bolo process servers, GigaStead private security ("Neighborhood Relations"), Overflow's ear-pieced greeters — so *where* you commit a crime changes *who* comes, before police ever do.

## 7. Ten Signature Missions

1. **Repo Girl** *(tutorial)* — Mari repossesses a Wedgeneer from a Harvest Hills driveway at 5 a.m., ducking an HOA drone and a coal-rolling bro-dozer via frontage roads and Texas turnarounds — the road grammar no other city has. **[DFW-unique]**
2. **Appreciate You** — Wade steals his own inventory off his own lot ahead of the auditors, smiling and waving at every camera, then torches the repo paperwork in a Whataburger-alike parking lot at 2 a.m.
3. **The Divinity Mile** — Illegal race inside the mile-wide leveed Div floodway under the Wishbone's arch, cops sealing the levee ramps one by one — a downtown river with no river in it. **[DFW-unique]**
4. **The Big Howdy** — Rolling armored-car hijack up all five levels of the interchange, Mari leapfrogging ramp to ramp while Wade runs blocker; exits via base-jump off the top ribbon. **[DFW-unique]**
5. **The Stallion** — Dub steals back a $2.2M cutting-horse stallion from a gated Shorelake estate mid-Futurity and smuggles him to the Chisholm Yards aboard the Muscadine vintage tourist railroad. **[DFW-unique]**
6. **Fair Warning** *(midpoint)* — Three protagonists, three jobs, one night at the Lone Star State Fair; the triple-cross converges at Tall Tom's boots; switch-showcase mission and the plot's hinge. **[DFW-unique]**
7. **Los Espejos** — Extract the whistleblower accountant from a mirrored tower by canal: water-taxi decoy, jet-ski chase through an office park's Venetian waterways at lunch hour.
8. **Gorilla Hail** — A dealership heist timed to a baseball-hail supercell: every alarm in the city already screaming, your getaway car denting in real time, roofing-scam hustlers working the block before the ice melts. **[DFW-unique]**
9. **Conservation Appeal** — Ice-storm blackout raid on GigaStead's private substation while T-GRID sheds load; ice physics flip the entire driving model; the only lit streets are generator-glow behind gates. **[DFW-unique]**
10. **The Groundbreaking** *(finale)* — Cattle drive up the tollway, hail-lashed escrow convoy, and a stolen ledger on the world's biggest video board as the funnel drops — three characters, one storm, one bell that never rings.

## 8. Five Satire Set Pieces (2026 targets)

1. **The Cattle-Horn Bell** — The Texchange IPO roadshow on Howdy Street ("Y'all Street," live July 2026): Wade teleprompters "Texas is the new Rome" to VCs in $900 ostrich boots; later the player shorts the listing through the in-game market — the satire is a mechanic, not a cutscene.
2. **Blessed & Zoned** — Overflow Fellowship's sermon series marketing Project Longhorn as providence, climaxing in the ticketed Christmas pageant heist: flying angels on wires, a thousand-voice choir, and live camels loose on the Golden Spur. Target: the machine, never the faith.
3. **The Decibel Hearing** — Hollow Creek retirees with clipboards vs. the HashHoller mine whose meter always reads one point under the limit; the player gathers true readings while the PR man calls the hum "the sound of freedom." The retirees are the heroes (punch-up rule 2).
4. **AGI in 18 Months, Again** — OmniMind's keynote inside Colossal Kidz Resort, Brisco: AGI announced quarterly since 2023, a "Stop the Slop" picket outside, and DJ Skye of 99.9 The Singularity hallucinating the weather over the PA while the player sabotages the demo, Lifeinvader-style.
5. **The Pecos Bullet Office** — A heist that must be cased from inside the high-speed-rail sales office (est. 2013), staffed by one aging true believer beneath a countdown frozen at "ARRIVING IN: 7 YEARS." NPCs measure all delays in Bullets.

**Playability rule for all five (design-DNA checklist item 17):** each target is attacked through a mechanic, not just text — the short-sale market for the Texchange, SeedFaith's streak economy as a hackable ledger, a working decibel meter, a sabotage sandbox at the keynote, and a countdown clock the player can finally, in one optional beat, set to zero.

## 9. Tone and Radio Identity

**Tone:** A Texan roasting Texas — affection is the license (punch-up rule 6). King of the Hill's sincerity wearing GTA's brass knuckles; heat-shimmer deadpan; courtesy as a threat display. Nobody in the Megaplex thinks they're in a satire; everybody's in on the joke except the institutions.

**Bark canon (per culture §12 — 80% of overheard dialogue draws from five topics):**
- "Hot enough for ya?" / "Wait five minutes." (weather, all year)
- "This is our year." (said with dead eyes, about the Rustlers, in any month)
- "Where'd you go to high school?" (Fort Verde interrogation opener)
- "What kind of coke you want?" "Dr. Zing." (correct answer)
- "That'll get done when the Bullet does." (the HSR as a unit of time)

**Radio is the class map.** The nine-station canon dial ships as designed (The Bull, Red Dirt Revival, Grind 105.7, The Warpath 1310, Patriot Talk 660, KSOL's Overflow Hour, La Jefa 104.5, Megaplex Public Radio, 99.9 The Singularity), plus one new station this pitch requires: **KRID 89.9 "The Ride"** — trail-ride soul, zydeco, southern soul, and Sunday gospel; Dub's dial, the Freedman's Ride's processional soundtrack, and the warmest voice on the spectrum. Protagonist defaults do characterization for free: Mari wakes to La Jefa and runs Grind at night (with D-Town Boogie throwback hours); Wade toggles The Warpath ("DAYS SINCE LAST CHAMPIONSHIP: 11,300") and Patriot Talk's generator ads; Dub keeps KRID on in the barn. Switching characters retunes the whole world's audio — the same freeway, three different Texases.

**The dial argues the plot.** As Project Longhorn advances, every station covers it in character: The Bull reads GigaStead spots between truck-month ads; Patriot Talk defends the grid the night it fails; La Jefa's accurate traffic reports track the player's own chaos; Megaplex Public Radio covers the water war calmly, like a nap; The Singularity's DJ Skye slowly becomes a witness for the prosecution. Traffic reports in authentic freeway-name cadence ("stall on Stemmons-alike at the Big Howdy, all five levels jammed") are the world's heartbeat — and after big missions, the dial remembers what you did (world-memory checklist item 14).

## 10. Open-World Expression

Between missions, the player lives inside the thesis — every free-roam verb accrues to one of two meters the story reads: **Respect** (earned in communities: the Bluff, the Yards, the trail) or **Goblin Value** (earned by assets: the HouseGoblin app reprices everything you own, hourly).

- **Wheels:** Slab Sunday cruises where slow earns respect and speed spends it; lowrider hop-offs; takeover nights with a task-force heat ladder and raidable impound lots; grudge cash races at the outlaw eighth-mile vs. sanctioned purses at the legal strip — with snitch tension between them.
- **Saddle:** Horseback traversal on 100 miles of Trinity Trails; hog trouble in the Div Bottoms; rodeo events at Cowtown Colosseum; bull riding and brawls at Big Bud's; the daily tourist cattle drive as Dub's paying job and cover.
- **Money:** The anti-Bolo counter-portfolio — buy back blocks, install tenants, watch Goblin Value fight your Respect; land flips and appraisal games (the playable S&L inheritance); toll-fee dread from the Grand Tollway Authority as ambient pressure; ParlayPal pushing parlays at 2 a.m.
- **Weather:** Storm chasing with StormCenter DEFCON; hail seasons that dent your garage's inventory and boom the body-shop economy; Conservation Appeal evenings when the streetlights die and the lawns fill with grills.
- **Texture:** HollerBurger runs as personality tests; dawn brisket lines that actually sell out; gun-show weekends; First Monday-style fence bazaar at the map's eastern edge; quinceañera escort jobs on Obregón; Friday nights when whole towns empty for high-school football and the burglary map lights up.

- **Per-protagonist free roam reads differently:** the same block offers Mari a repo contract, Wade a booster handshake, and Dub a shortcut no car can take. Random events pay forward across leads (the stranded rancher Mari tows in Act 1 lends Dub thirty horses in Act 3 — the world remembering kindness, design-DNA §4).
- **Seasonal world events on the calendar, not the script:** the Fair's 24 days, HS football Fridays and December playoffs, Stock Show in January, hail season, Tết at the Asia-plaza district, Diwali fireworks over Jubilee Park, the Juneteenth ride — the map has a year planner and the story borrows it.

Freedom serves the theme because the sandbox asks the player the same question the plot asks the trio: *are you rooting here, or extracting?* The world's two ledgers keep the score either way — and the ending you're offered leans on which one you fed.

## 11. The Only-in-DFW Test

- **Two downtowns.** Los Santos and Vice City are monocentric; the Megaplex is a dumbbell — two skylines, two police forces, two self-images, 32 real miles of grudge between them. The ensemble structure *is* the geography: no second downtown, no triangle, no story.
- **The road grammar.** Frontage roads, Texas turnarounds, toll gantries, a five-level interchange as a civic monument — chase choreography no other city's streets can perform, and this script's biggest missions are built on it.
- **Weather as act structure.** No ocean, no mountains — the sky is the terrain: heat dome, gorilla hail, ice storm, supercell finale. Vice City has one hurricane note; we have four seasons of dramaturgy.
- **Cattle in traffic.** A daily longhorn drive through a working district, a horse culture that predates the freeways, and a finale cavalry of trail riders on a tollway. Unstageable anywhere else in America.
- **The costume war.** "All hat, no cattle" is a class system you can *see*: boot wear, hat crease, truck-bed scratches as an authenticity stat. LA satirizes fame; Miami satirizes vice; only DFW satirizes *performed frontier virtue* — and its native crime genre is white-collar (S&L, affinity fraud, land flips), which is the heist innovation this pitch runs on.
- **Traffic tells the class story.** The 5 a.m. work-truck migration north and the 6 p.m. reverse flow (culture canon §8), toll lanes pricing the poor onto clogged free roads, a stadium city with no bus — the commute *is* the inequality, legible from the driver's seat. Los Santos has traffic; the Megaplex has a caste system with lane assignments.
- **The gospel.** Megachurch campuses as the suburbs' true skyscrapers, a stock exchange with a cattle-horn bell, compute ranches drinking the aquifer — growth-as-religion isn't a theme imposed on this map; it's the map.

**The one-sentence version:** Los Santos satirizes wanting to be *seen*; Vice City satirizes wanting to *feel*; the Megaplex satirizes wanting to *grow* — and only a flat, two-hearted, storm-lit boomtown where cattle share the road with IPO convoys can stage that argument.

## 12. Risks and Rating Notes

- **Three protagonists vs. solo-dev canon.** Design-DNA §3 says "never three" for this team size. This pitch spends the risk knowingly and mitigates: one shared map and faction cast; **Rashomon replays** (key missions re-playable from a second lead's perspective — one level built, two stories told); interlocked missions where leads appear in each other's scenes so VO and vignettes do double duty; staged build (vertical slice = Mari alone; Wade joins in the Act-2 build; Dub last). **Graceful fallback:** fold Wade into a non-playable tragicomic fixer and ship a Mari/Dub duo — the class triangle survives as a two-hander with the suburbs as setting rather than lens.
- **GTA 6 shadow.** Launching genre expectations reset Nov 19, 2026 (Vice City, duo structure). Differentiation is the whole pitch: geography (dumbbell metro, weather system), crime genre (white-collar/land), cast (a 58-year-old Black cowboy and a 26-year-old lowrider driver are leads no Rockstar game has shipped).
- **Sensitivity load (all governed by binding punch-up rules).** Megachurch arc satirizes the machine — payouts, PR, the jet — never abuse or survivors; no congregation is the joke. The Freedman's Ride, the Bluff, and every community in culture-canon §9 get specificity and dignity — consultants and sensitivity reads budgeted, "missions about land and inheritance, not street-crime clichés." Border material stays procurement-only if used at all. Dealey Plaza has no parody analog — real tragedy is not set dressing; the downtown grid simply doesn't quote it. Tall Tom's fire history stays an affectionate running gag; he does not burn on camera. No real living person: Bidwell, Kessler, Everly are composites (≥3 sources + invention).
- **Rating: M (17+) / PEGI 18.** Violence (gunplay, no torture-adjacent content — we decline GTA 5's "By the Book" mistake per design-DNA §7), strong language, drug references (THC gray-market satire), simulated gambling (ParlayPal is the villain of its own storyline; expect gambling descriptors and regional flags — no real-money mechanics anywhere). Animal welfare: horses and cattle can't be harmed by design; the finale herd is chaos, not casualties.
- **Audio scope.** Ten radio stations imply either licensing budget (unrealistic solo) or original/commissioned music: plan for AI-assisted original beds with human-composed flagships per station, and talk stations (Warpath, Patriot Talk, KMPX, Overflow Hour) carrying satire cheaply — talk is the highest joke-per-dollar format. VO plan: barks and talk radio first, protagonist VO last (text-first narrative until funded).
- **Scope sanity.** The pitch's systemic asks (two-meter reputation, market shorting, weather acts) all reuse core sim layers already in the design DNA; the expensive novelties are the horse lane and three wardrobes. Cut order if needed: Rashomon replays → hop-off minigame depth → third ending variant. The triangle is the last thing to cut, because it's the pitch.
- **Checklist self-score (design-DNA §8, honest):** strong on items 3–5 (ring roads, greatest-hits compression, editorializing map), 7 (prep→execute), 10 (fail-forward low point), 14 (dial memory), 16–18 (thesis, playable satire, radio-as-worldbuilding); at-risk items are 9 (cheap retry across three-prong finale — needs per-prong checkpoints) and 19 (full side-content taxonomy — hobbies tier is the thinnest and should borrow rodeo/dominoes/fishing first).
