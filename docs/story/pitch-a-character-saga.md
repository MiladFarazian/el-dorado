# Pitch A — Character Saga: "COMIN' DOWN"

**Lens:** grounded, character-first crime saga. Prestige-TV energy — *Hell or High Water* × *Friday Night Lights* × the warmth of *King of the Hill*, running on GTA systems. The satire is the water; the heart is the arc. Crime is economically inevitable, never aspirational.
**Status:** story pitch, v1 (July 2026). Grounded in `/docs/research/` canon: dfw-geography, dfw-culture, satire-targets, gta-design-dna.

---

## 1. Working Title + Alternates

**COMIN' DOWN** — slab slang for showing out slow ("comin' down, swangin'"), and the literal arc: a man coming down in the world, learning how to land.

- Alt 1: **THE FULL EIGHT** — the eight seconds a bull rider must survive; the protagonist held 7.9.
- Alt 2: **ALL HAT, NO CATTLE** — Fort Worth's verdict on Dallas, and the game's verdict on the boom.

**World naming** (canon's provisional names adopted; additions proposed here, world doc locks them):
- Dallas → **Dorado** | Fort Worth → **Fort Verde** | the Metroplex → **the Megaplex** (all per satire canon, kept)
- Frisco → **Brisco**, Plano → **Blando**, Arlington → **Middlington**, Celina → **Salvation**, Southlake → **Shorelake** (kept)
- NEW — Oak Cliff → **Cedar Cliff** (locals say "the Cliff," preserving real speech rhythm)
- NEW — Trinity River / floodway / Great Trinity Forest → **the Redemption River / the Redemption Channel / the Bottoms**. A river named for salvation that the city walled off and forgot: the theme in hydrology.
- NEW — Joppa/Tenth Street-inspired freedman's-town neighborhood (fictional composite, never 1:1) → **Gilead Bottoms**, "the last horse lots in the floodplain."
- NEW — Mesquite → **Mesquital**, Grand Prairie → **Grand Prairieview**, Weatherford horse country → **Wetherford**, Harry Hines Blvd → **Barry Vines Blvd**, Jefferson Blvd → **Juárez Boulevard**.
- The High Five interchange keeps its name — it's vernacular, not a brand, and no other city could claim it.

---

## 2. Logline

A broke ex-bull-rider repossesses cars across a booming Texas metroplex — until he discovers the land flips funding his family's survival are erasing his own neighborhood, and turns his tow hook on the boom itself. *(34 words)*

---

## 3. Era and Season

**Now — one calendar year, spring to fall, at the boom's high noon (a lightly fictionalized 2026).**

Why: every satire target in canon is live *this minute* — the Texchange rang its cattle-horn bell in July, GigaStead-class data centers are drinking the aquifer, the grid begs for conservation while server barns hum, Bolo Capital owns your vet and your AC guy. A period piece would let the audience off the hook. And the DFW calendar is a ready-made three-act clock:

- **Act 1 = supercell season (April–June).** Gorilla hail totals 40,000 cars; the roofer-and-wrecker economy erupts. The storm is the inciting incident *and* the regional economy.
- **Act 2 = the furnace (July–August).** 41 days over 100°. Conservation Appeals, brownouts, generator hum in the rich zip codes. Heat as narrative pressure.
- **Act 3 = Fair season (late September–October).** The Lone Star State Fair: 24 days, two million visitors, a 55-foot talking cowboy, and one out-of-season storm nobody forecast.
- **Epilogue = first ice storm.** The Megaplex glazed silent. Aftermath weather.

The sky is the map's terrain (canon §6, geography). So the sky is also the story's structure.

## 4. Protagonist

**Booker "Book" Reyes, 34.** Cedar Cliff born, Gilead Bottoms raised. Father's side: a Black trail-ride family out of the Best Southwest suburbs — the tradition of the 1-in-4 cowboys the tourists never heard about. Mother's side: a Cedar Cliff charro family off Juárez Boulevard — lowriders on Sunday, escaramuza cousins, a Virgen airbrushed on the shop wall. Book is both cowboy traditions in one body, which makes him the only man in the Megaplex who can walk the Fort Verde Stockyards, a Candyland slab cruise, and a Wetherford cutting-horse barn without changing clothes — just hats.

**Background.** Rode bulls out of Mesquital and the Cowtown circuit from 16 to 29. Held one for 7.9 seconds at the Mesquital Championship Rodeo; the eighth second cost him a hip, a title, and the sponsor money that was going to clear his grandmother Miss Earlene's six acres in Gilead Bottoms. Five years later he drives a wrecker for Longhorn Wrecker & Recovery on Barry Vines Blvd — repossessions, impound runs, predatory hooks off frontage lots. Then Bolo Capital buys Longhorn, installs quotas and an app, and the job stops being a job and becomes extraction with a logo. The stable's roof goes in the April hailstorm. The insurance denies. The tax appraisal doubles — because something is quietly buying the floodplain. Crime isn't a temptation; it's the only line item left.

**Want:** clear title on Miss Earlene's six acres and the stable reopened — and, privately, one sanctioned ride to retire on his own terms instead of on a stretcher.
**Need:** to learn that a rigged ledger can't be beaten by playing ledger — and that his inheritance was never the parcel. It's the riders, the horses, the Sunday ride itself. A bull rider's whole discipline is holding on; Book needs to learn what to let go of, and when.

**Playstyle identity — four verbs, one man:**
- **The Hook.** The wrecker is his weapon, wallet, and calling card: steal-by-tow, drag ATMs, rip gates, snatch a getaway car *with the driver still in it*. Every parked car in the Megaplex becomes a verb.
- **The Saddle.** A horse layer no GTA has: trail rides as social space, the Bottoms and the Redemption Channel as horseback country, hog trouble in the forest, and the finale ride.
- **The Slow Lane.** Slab logic inverts GTA: in Candyland territory, *speed loses respect*. Comin' down at 15 mph with the trunk popped is the flex. A driving game that rewards patience is a new feeling.
- **The Paper.** Mid-game, Book's crime graduates to DFW's true brand — titles, appraisals, straw buyers, shell LLCs. White-collar fraud as playable heist (canon culture §11).
- **Special ability: "The Full Eight."** Eight seconds of bull-rider time-dilation — driving, riding, or hanging off a moving trailer. His trauma, weaponized; the meter is a bull rope.

**Why players will love inhabiting him:** the competence fantasy of the hook; the dry Texas wit ("bless your heart" as a threat); a moral gradient the player actually steers; and the rarest thing in open-world crime — a protagonist with somewhere warm to come home to. Miss Earlene's porch, Sunday zydeco rides, the tamalada at Christmas. GTA gives you safehouses. Book has a *home*, and the entire game is about whether it survives him.

**Supporting crew (systemic companion layer per design-DNA §3 — one protagonist, no switching):**
- **Miss Earlene Reyes, 77** — matriarch of the Bottomland Riders; the stable is hers; the game's conscience, never its victim.
- **Ambrose "Sudden" Slaughter, 71** — Black rodeo legend, Book's old mentor, keeps a $2M memory and a longer grudge against the Drover Group.
- **Randy "Squall" Hutto, 38** — storm-chasing roofer, scanner poet, first through every hailstorm; comic relief with a ParlayPal problem.
- **Marisol "Sol" Reyes, 26** — Book's cousin; title-office clerk turned the crew's paper genius; the escalation engine and eventually its loudest doubter.
- **Officer Dena Ruiz, MART Transit PD** — the cop gone sideways, working the one downtown rail tunnel every line shares; foil, informant, and mirror.

## 5. Three-Act Arc

**ACT 1 — HAIL MONEY (spring).** Cold open: Book in the chute, 7.9 seconds, five years ago — a playable prologue that teaches grip, balance, and loss in ninety seconds. Cut to now: repo tutorial off a Shorelake driveway. The April supercell hits; the metro becomes a body-shop economy overnight; Bolo Capital's new quotas turn Book's route predatory. When Longhorn impounds half of Gilead Bottoms' hail-totaled cars — including his neighbors' — Book flips: a night raid liberates twelve cars from his own employer's yard, convoyed over the arch bridge into the empty Redemption Channel. First windfall. He's in with Candyland C.C., towing cars that need to disappear, and it feels — briefly — like justice with a paycheck.

**ACT 2 — PAPER (summer).** The furnace. A Conservation Appeal blackout puts Miss Earlene in the Medical District; the debt clock starts. Sol shows Book where the real money hides: not in cars, in *chains of title*. The crew runs the I-30 playbook on the northern frontier — straw buyers, cooked appraisals, the same Harvest Hills lot flipped three times before lunch — selling the boom back to the boom, Overflow Fellowship's church-plant real-estate arm buying high on God's behalf.

**MIDPOINT REVERSAL:** Book closes his biggest flip and traces the buyer's shell LLC — through three cutouts — to the entity quietly assembling Gilead Bottoms for GigaStead's "Project Alamo" compute campus. The land man, Garland "Gar" Voss, didn't infiltrate Book's operation. He *founded* it: the disappearing cars, the seed money, the "friendly" title contacts — all Voss, using the Bottoms' own son to clear the Bottoms. Book isn't being eaten by the machine. He's a component. The back half of Act 2 is Book trying to buy back what he helped sell: the Wetherford stallion heist during the Futurity, the raid on Overflow's tithing ledger while its leadership publicly knife-fights over the founder's retirement payout. Each score is bigger; each closing costs him someone — Sol walks, Sudden stops answering, Candyland brands him management.

**ACT 3 — FAIR SEASON (fall).** The city votes Project Alamo through during fair week — ribbon-cutting scheduled at the fair's GigaStead pavilion, Tall Tom presiding. Book goes loud: infiltrate the Lone Star State Fair on its final night, lift Voss's deal archive — the entire fraudulent title chain, his own crimes included — from the pavilion vault, out through two million people.

**FINALE SET PIECE — "THE LAST RIDE."** Mid-heist, the sirens start: an out-of-season supercell, wall cloud over the midway, the fair evacuating in real time. Book escapes the pavilion on Sudden's stallion — through the stock barns, down a midway of abandoned funnel-cake stands, past Tall Tom catching fire for the third time in his life ("he doesn't like to talk about it"), out the Deco gates as the tornado drops. Final confrontation at the half-built GigaStead campus in the Bottoms as the Redemption River rises into the server halls — the storm repossessing the boom, with the repo man holding the paper. **Box art:** a man on horseback mid-midway, burning 55-foot cowboy and ferris wheel behind him, green-black sky above.

**Endings (player choice, GTA-DNA finale rule):**
1. **Take the Money.** Ride off rich; the Bottoms sells; the radio spends the epilogue calling it a miracle.
2. **Burn the Paper.** Torch the title chain — the Bottoms survives in legal limbo; Book keeps nothing but the stable and Sunday.
3. **The Full Eight.** Hand everything to the feds — Bolo, Overflow, GigaStead, himself — the S&L-style trial of the decade. Book finally holds on for the whole eight. It costs exactly what holding on always costs.

## 6. Six Factions

| Faction | Turf | Aesthetic | Narrative role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bottomland Riders** | Gilead Bottoms, the Redemption Channel & forest, southern Dorado | Trail-ride Americana: horse trailers, zydeco speakers, Juneteenth reds, mud-caked Ariats that earned it | Home. The heart faction and moral center; what every other faction is ultimately fighting over |

| **Overflow Fellowship** | Blando/Shorelake campus belt + a satellite in south Dorado | Arena rigging, fog machines, tithing-app streak flames, armed greeters | The imploding faith conglomerate: laundering temptation, follow-the-tithe missions, radio-news serial |

## 7. Ten Signature Missions

1. **"Hook and Ladder"** — tutorial repo: lift a Steed Longrider "Baron Brisket" out of a Stonebridle Ranch cul-de-sac while the HOA drone livestreams you to Porchlight.
2. **"Gorilla Hail"** *(only-in-DFW: supercell as mission space)* — race Squall's roofing rig INTO the storm to canvas ahead of rival chasers; baseball hail dents your hood in real time and every dent is money.
3. **"The Yard"** *(only-in-DFW: the Redemption/Trinity floodway)* — liberate twelve impounded hail cars from your own employer, convoy over the white arch bridge down into the mile-wide empty floodway, headlights off, skyline behind.
4. **"Comin' Down"** — earn Candyland respect in a reverse-race: a slab cruise where speed *loses* points, ending in a takeover as the Street Racing Task Force escalates tier by tier.
5. **"The High Five"** *(only-in-DFW: the five-level interchange)* — board a Bolo car-carrier mid-interchange and decouple its deck on the ramps, seized slabs rolling home down five separate levels of ribbon concrete.
6. **"Book of Numbers"** — clone Overflow's tithing ledger from the pageant tech booth and exit through the live-camel processional during the flying-angel cue.
7. **"Futurity"** *(only-in-DFW: Stockyards cattle drive)* — swap a $2.2M cutting stallion in the warm-up pens at the Fort Verde futurity, then ride out through Exchange Avenue hidden inside the twice-daily longhorn drive.
8. **"Appraisal Day"** — the I-30 scandal as gameplay: flip one Harvest Hills lot three times before lunch, ferrying a crooked appraiser down the tollway against the county recorder's office hours; the timer is bureaucracy itself.
9. **"Conservation Appeal"** — blackout night: the Bottoms is dark, GigaStead hums at 72 dB; tap the compute ranch's private substation and re-light the neighborhood while Patriot Talk 660 praises the grid's "market signal."
10. **"Fair Game" / "The Last Ride"** *(only-in-DFW: the State Fair + tornado)* — the finale: vault heist in the GigaStead pavilion, sirens, evacuating midway, horseback escape past a burning Tall Tom as the wall cloud drops.

*(Bench: "Hog Wild" — a trunk problem needs to disappear in the Bottoms forest at night, between feral hogs and a game warden; "End of the Line" — tail a witness through the one downtown tunnel all four MART lines share.)*

## 8. Five Satire Set Pieces (2026 targets)

1. **The Project Alamo Groundbreaking** *(AI data-center land rush)* — valet the ribbon-cutting where GigaStead fills its koi pond the same week Salvation's wells run dry; the gift bags contain commemorative rain barrels.
2. **The Texchange Bell** *(Y'all Street, live July 2026)* — work security at Howdy Street's cattle-horn opening bell among VCs in $900 ostrich boots saying "Texas is the new Rome" into podcast mics; lift a ledger drive during the barbacoa reception.
3. **The Pecos Bullet Showroom** *(high-speed-rail purgatory)* — repossess the scale-model train from a gleaming sales office (est. 2013) staffed by one aging true believer; the billboard outside still reads "ARRIVING IN: 7 YEARS."
4. **"NOW LEGAL / NEVER MIND"** *(THC whiplash)* — run a delivery for the Smoke 'Em If You Got 'Em vape barn as the product's legality flips twice mid-route via executive order; the signage changes while you're parked.
5. **The Margin Call at the Ego Dome** *(sports-betting purgatory)* — extract Squall from a Rustlers game after his ParlayPal "prediction contracts" get margin-called at 2 a.m.; the app is the villain, its push notifications score the chase.

## 9. Tone and Radio Identity

**Tone:** *Hell or High Water* sincerity in the foreground, GTA satire in the set dressing, *Friday Night Lights* warmth at home base. The camera loves the flat horizon and the 40,000-foot anvil; the writing loves people. Rule of thumb from canon punch-up law: **the neighborhood is never the joke — the paperwork is.** Texans are in on it. Violence has weight; Book is dangerous, not a cartoon; the comedy lives on the dial, the billboards, and in what institutions say about themselves.

**Radio:** the nine-station dial from satire canon, fully adopted (The Bull, Red Dirt Revival, Grind 105.7, The Warpath 1310, Patriot Talk 660, KSOL Overflow Hour, La Jefa 104.5, Megaplex Public Radio, 99.9 The Singularity) — plus one addition this story earns:

- **Trail Ride Traxx 88.1** — low-power zydeco/southern-soul station broadcast from a shed in Gilead Bottoms; Sunday ride dedications, Miss Earlene's minute of community news, the only station that will ever say Book's real name on air.

Book's dial *is* his characterization: Grind 105.7 in the wrecker, La Jefa in the lowrider, Trail Ride Traxx on horseback, and — alone, late, windows up — Red Dirt Revival, because the failed cowboy still believes the songs. News interstitials serialize the Overflow collapse, the Project Alamo vote, and the player's own chaos (world-memory rule, design-DNA §7.5).

## 10. Open-World Expression

Between missions, the whole map argues the theme — **extraction versus stewardship** — and every free-roam system asks which one you're doing:

- **The Hook economy.** Freelance wrecker board: legit repos, gray-zone snatches, black-market steal-to-order. Same verb, sliding morality, visible consequences (Porchlight posts, districts that remember).
- **The paper ladder.** Buy property to *flip* (fast money, neighborhood decay you can watch) or to *hold* (slow rent, the block visibly steadies). The endgame sink is a literal answer to GTA 5's broken economy: real estate is our satire, so real estate is our sink.
- **The horse life.** Stable chores, hog problems in the Bottoms, Sunday trail rides that function as social hub, intel exchange, and the one place heat never follows.
- **The slab garage.** Elbows, candy, trunk marquees; cruise-slow respect scoring; takeover nights that migrate one exit down the frontage road when the law shows.
- **Storm season.** Weather is an economy: chase hail with Squall, sell tarps ahead of the scam roofers, or loot the evacuated tollway. Sirens empty streets for anyone bold enough to use them.
- **Rituals on the calendar.** First Monday flea market as fence hub, Friday rodeo at the Coliseum, gun-show weekends, quinceañera escorts on Juárez, December playoff caravans — the year is the content.
- **Freedom serves theme** because both economies pay: the player who strips the Megaplex for parts and the player who quietly re-roofs Gilead Bottoms are both playing correctly — and the world, the radio, and Miss Earlene's porch will tell each of them, specifically, who they became.

## 11. The Only-in-DFW Test

- **Los Santos has an ocean and mountains; the Megaplex has sky and paper.** Our terrain drama is a supercell, our biggest heists are title chains — a crime economy (S&L legacy, appraisal fraud, affinity scams) no coastal GTA has ever made playable.
- **The dumbbell.** Two genuine downtowns with opposite souls, 30 miles of story between them. Vice City has one skyline and a beach; we have a rivalry you can drive.
- **Frontage-road grammar.** One-way service roads, Texas turnarounds, commerce ON the freeway's shoulder — a chase-and-commerce geometry that exists nowhere else in America, and our whole traffic feel depends on it.
- **A protagonist only this map can produce.** Black trail-ride clubs + charro families + slab Sundays + an outlaw drag strip + the Stockyards: Book's four verbs (hook, saddle, slow lane, paper) each come from a real DFW subculture, and no other city has all four.
- **Slow is the flex.** Slab culture inverts the core GTA verb. A driving game where crawling at 15 mph earns respect could only be set here.
- **The set pieces are non-portable.** A five-level interchange chase, a heist hidden inside a longhorn cattle drive, a finale where a tornado evacuates a 24-day state fair around you — move any of these to Los Santos and they stop making sense.
- **The villain is the boom itself.** Y'all Street, compute ranches, megachurch REITs, PE wreckers — 2026's national economy is *headquartered here*. This isn't a city where the satire could happen; it's where it is happening.

## 12. Risks and Rating Notes

- **Rating: M (17+).** Violence, strong language, drugs (THC-whiplash satire), simulated gambling (ParlayPal — depicted as predatory, never rewarded mechanically), crime. No sexual violence, no torture set piece (explicit lesson from GTA 5's "By the Book," design-DNA §7.4).
- **Displacement storytelling.** Gilead Bottoms is a fictional composite (Joppa + Tenth Street inspirations), never a 1:1 map of a real community; residents are protagonists, never props. Canon rule: South Dallas material is the moral center — dignity and specificity, or cut it.
- **Church satire discipline.** Overflow Fellowship targets the machine — payouts, PR, jets, seed-faith economics. Abuse and survivors are never material, per binding punch-up rules. Legal review on any beat adjacent to the real 2024–26 scandal arc.
- **No real living persons.** Colt Bidwell, Pastor Everly, Gar Voss are composites (≥3 sources + invention). Watch proximity traps: the grandmother figure must not echo any real living Fort Worth matriarch; rodeo legends must not map to living riders.
- **Disaster sensitivity.** Storms are fictional events; never recreate the 2019 EF3's path or any real casualty event. Tornado finale reads as nature-vs-boom, not disaster tourism.
- **Cultural authenticity load.** Black trail-ride, charro, slab, and lowrider cultures require paid community consultants and voice casting from the communities — this is the pitch's soul and its highest execution risk.
- **Scope (solo dev).** The horse layer, hail-deformation, and calendar-season simulation are the three most expensive systems. De-scope ladder: (1) seasons become act-scripted weather states, not simulation; (2) horse layer confined to the Bottoms + finale; (3) hail damage limited to player vehicle + scripted lots. The Hook and the Paper systems are non-negotiable — they ARE the game.
- **Market timing.** GTA 6 ships November 19, 2026 into Vice City glamour. Our counter-position is the whole pitch: their crime is aspirational neon; ours is a man trying to keep six acres. Do not compete on spectacle density; compete on place, warmth, and the only map in games where the sky is the boss fight.
