If a name isn't in here, it isn't canon yet; register it here before it ships in a doc, a mission, or a texture. Retired variants are listed so nobody reuses them by accident. The two D-005 conflicts are settled below (ADOPTED provisional — Milad may veto; see `docs/decisions.md`).

## 0. The Laws of Naming

1. **Parody, never verbatim.** No real brand, landmark, or institution keeps its name. The only verbatim survivals allowed: **Texas** itself (the satire is *about* Texas and "Texans in on the joke" requires saying so — the state is named; the map doc's "implied, unnamed" option is retired), pure descriptive genericisms (Exchange Avenue, Texas turnaround, First Monday, the Stock Show, "the Futurity"), and earned street vernacular ("Cowtown," "the Cliff," "the Bottoms," "Big Dorado").
2. **The Texan test.** A name must sound *found*, not *written* — like locals, surveyors, or a bored traffic reporter coined it, never a writers' room. When a clever name and a plain name tie, the plain one wins.
3. **No real living people.** Composites only, ≥3 sources + invention (binding, satire canon §4). Watch proximity traps (no real matriarchs, no living riders).
4. **One joke per mechanism.** The "+N one-upmanship" gag belongs to Eight Flags Over Middlington alone. The "-inity rhyme" slot died with Divinity River. Don't re-run a retired name's joke under a new spelling.
5. **Punch-up rules apply to names too** (satire canon §4, binding): institutions are targets; communities, victims, and migrants never are.
6. **Retired ≠ deleted.** Retired variants below are quarantined: don't reuse them for anything else — they're documented precisely so they stay dead.

## 1. D-005 Rulings (the two open conflicts)

### 1a. The river → **the Threefork River**

**Ruling: the Threefork River** (with the West and Clear Forks meeting at Fort Verde, per map canon). The test is whether a Texan would have left this name on a map, and Threefork is the only candidate that passes it cold: the real Trinity is made of West, Clear, and Elm Forks, Texans genuinely name their water by its forks, and "Threefork" reads like an 1850s surveyor wrote it in a ledger and nobody ever had a reason to change it. The Trinity echo survives *literally* — three forks in one river IS the trinity, told with a straight face — so locals still clock the parody without the name winking at them. "Redemption River" fails the native test in the opposite direction: it's a writers' room naming the theme, and a river that says the theme out loud cheapens every scene staged on it. "Divinity" is the cleverest of the three (it rhymes the theology), but the map is already carrying Salvation, Gilead Bottoms, Overflow Fellowship, and SeedFaith — one more theological name over-salts the register, and the strongest version of the theme is the quiet one: *the boom renames everything it touches, but the river keeps its plain surveyor's name, because the land was here before the gospel.* Practical clincher: ratified map canon already builds on Threefork — the Threefork Floodway ("the Big Empty"), the Threefork Bottoms, and the **TRE** (Threefork Rail Express), whose real-world acronym joke dies under any other name.

**Consequential renames:** Pitch A's "Redemption Channel" → **the Threefork Floodway** (vernacular: "the Big Empty" / "the Channel"); map POI "Trinity Gate" → **Threefork Gate**; Pitch C's "Trinity Trails" → **the Threefork Trails**. **Gilead Bottoms is untouched** — it never needed the river to rhyme with it, and the pairing is stronger for being unforced.
**Retired:** Redemption River, the Redemption Channel (A); Divinity River, "the Div," the Div Bottoms (C); the Trinidad, the Baptism (map alternates); Trinity Gate (map, internal inconsistency).

### 1b. The High Five interchange → **the Big Howdy**

**Ruling: the Big Howdy.** Pitch A is right that "the High Five" is vernacular — DFW really does nickname its interchanges (the real Mixmaster proves the register) — but that argument cuts the other way here: in this world the signature interchanges are the *monuments* (the Tangle, the Knot, the Braid, the Canyon all carry coined names in ratified map canon), and one verbatim real name in that set is a seam, in a world where even the vernacular "Metroplex" got parodied to "the Megaplex." Between the two coinages, the Big Howdy sounds found and the High Ten sounds written: "High Ten" is the same one-upmanship arithmetic as "Eight Flags Over Middlington — we added two flags to win," and the world only gets to tell that joke once (Eight Flags owns it). The Big Howdy carries its own image instead of borrowing the real name's: five levels of strangers waving at people they will never meet — warmth with teeth, sized in the "Big D"/"Big Empty" register of Texas grandiosity — and it's exactly what a traffic reporter would be calling it by year two, which is how the real High Five got named. The authenticity judge endorsed it, and it survives contact with a road sign, a traffic hit ("stall on the Big Howdy, all five levels jammed"), a mission title, and box art.

**Consequential renames:** Pitch A's mission "The High Five" retitles to **"The Big Howdy"** (Pitch C already staged its armored-car mission there under that name).
**Retired:** the High Ten (map); the verbatim "High Five" (A's keep-it option).

## 13. Reconciliations Beyond D-005 (one-line reasons; all WB, veto-able)

1. **Cedar Cliff** over Oak Bluff (map) / The Bluff (C): the ratified spine says "Cedar Cliff born," and "the Cliff" preserves the real speech rhythm — the vernacular is the load-bearing part. Map region 4 relabels accordingly; the streetcar becomes the **Cliff Streetcar**.
2. **Juárez Boulevard** over Zaragoza (map) / Obregón (C): ratified spine text; Juárez is the statesman Mexican-American main streets actually get named for.
3. **Barry Vines Boulevard** over Harmon Hines (map): "Hines" kept a real surname verbatim; Barry Vines carries the rhyme without it.
4. **Rendezvous Tower ("the Lollipop")**: proper name from the ratified C graft (keeps Reunion's French-colony echo); nickname from map. Roundup over-cowboys a glass downtown.
5. **Cattleman's Trust Tower ("the Green Light")** over Bank of Dorado Plaza ("the Green Giant"): grafted set names the Green Light; "Green Giant" collides with a real food trademark; "Bank of Dorado" is find-replace parody.
6. **The Neon Mustang** over the Red Stallion: grafted set; keeps map's Magnate Building.
7. **DVI ("the Third City")** over Midfield International: DVI is named in ratified D-004 text itself; "Midfield" survives as tower/traffic slang so the judge-endorsed word still lives on the dial.
8. **The Brandyards** over the Chisholm Yards: ratified map region name, and Chisholm is already triple-booked (Chisholm Pike, Chisholm Christian U).
9. **Wild Wanda's** over Big Bud's Texas; **Drover Coliseum** over Cowtown Colosseum: map canon; Drover ties the venue to the Drover Group's myth economy. "Cowtown" stays pure vernacular for Fort Verde itself.
10. **Deep Elm** over Deep Ash; **Lone Star Fairgrounds / Cottonwood Bowl** over Jubilee Park / the Boll; **Dorado North Tollpike** over Golden Spur Tollway: ratified map canon in each case.
11. **Trail Ride Traxx 88.1** over KRID 89.9 "The Ride": story-direction adopted Traxx by name; one trail-ride station only.
12. **Bottomland Riders** over the Freedman's Ride; **Candyland C.C.** over the Coronado Car Club: ratified A factions; C's were the same archetypes wearing different names.
13. **Project Alamo** over Project Longhorn: ratified midpoint; also keeps "Longhorn" free for Longhorn Wrecker, Longhorn Dynamics, and the Superspeedway (Texas genuinely over-uses Longhorn; four is honest, five is noise).
14. **Threefork Gate** over Trinity Gate: no "Trinity" may survive anywhere in-world once the river is the Threefork.
15. **Grand Prairieview** retired: D-004 fused Grand Prairie into Middlington; the name has no referent left on the map.
16. **Wetherford** adopted (A) and placed on the Tangles' western horse fringe — the map cut Weatherford the town, but the story's cutting-horse country needs a name.
17. **The state is Texas**, named: the Texchange, the fair, and half the billboard canon already say so; retiring map §3's "implied, unnamed" option.
