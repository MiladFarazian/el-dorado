## D-009 · 2026-07-30 · Carjacking: the player vehicle is a role, not an object
**Decision:** Any vehicle can be taken on foot with E. Implementing it required changing what "the player's car" means: `main.vehicle` is now the vehicle you are *currently driving* (a role that moves), and a new `main.own_vehicle` holds the Longhorn Wrecker you *own*. All key-swaps go through one funnel, `main.set_player_vehicle()`. Consequences ratified with it:
- **The Hook is equipment.** Only a profile declaring `tow_boom: true` can tow — the wrecker, and nothing else. Jack a sedan and the repo job is on hold until you walk back to your truck. (This also changes Tab behaviour: cycling into the Slab or the Brisket now loses towing, correctly.)
- **Traffic cars are converted, not possessed.** Ambient traffic is frozen-kinematic props with nothing to drive; a jacked one is replaced in the same frame by a real `RaycastVehicle` inheriting its position, heading and lane speed. New profile: **Sunbelt Vantage** (`sedan.json`); traffic's black lifted truck becomes the Baron Brisket it already looked like.
- **Crime is priced by who was in it.** Empty car: free. Somebody's car: +2 stars and the driver leaves by the far door. Marked cruiser: +3 stars, it leaves the police roster and group, and its siren is killed on theft (a wail that never ends stops being funny) while the lightbar keeps strobing.
- **County General returns you to YOUR truck**, not to whatever you had stolen — otherwise dying two districts from the wrecker strands the repo loop permanently.
- **Junkers stay un-jackable.** They are dead weight with no keys, which is the entire reason the job is to hook them. The prompt says so.
**Rationale:** The game is an open-world crime game in which the player could only enter one specific truck. Carjacking is the genre's defining verb, and the ownership split is what lets it exist without dissolving the repo-man fiction — Book can steal anything, but only his own rig can *work*. Scope guard: abandoned stolen cars are real physics vehicles, so they are swept on distance + a hard cap of 5.
**Verification:** both CI gates green (smoke byte-identical at moved=40.5m/16.7m/s; clean 900-iteration boot), plus a temporary 26-check headless integration harness covering both theft paths, since neither shipped gate exercises input. Harness found and fixed a real defect: `vehicle_audio.on_vehicle_changed` assumed the old vehicle was always freed (true when Tab was the only way to change cars), so every abandoned car idled its engine loop forever and leaked it at quit.
**Owner:** Technical Director + Game Systems Designer. **Status: ADOPTED** — Milad may veto any of the consequences above, particularly the Tab/tow change.

## D-010 · 2026-08-01 · Armed response: the escalation ladder
**Decision:** Police use lethal force, priced by heat. The ladder: **heat 0–1 chase and ram only** (gray work stays survivable — the strip teaches, it doesn't punish); **heat 2+ cruisers fire on the ride** (they want the vehicle stopped); **heat 3 they also fire on the man on foot**. Mechanics ratified with it:
- **Fair-warning fire model:** shots require continuous line of sight held through a 0.75 s windup, then one shot per cruiser per 1.2 s; accuracy falls with distance and with both parties' speed. No minimum range — parking next to a cop car is never a safe zone.
- **Vehicle hull** is a per-profile JSON tunable (`hull`: wrecker 130, Brisket 140, Interceptor 110, Slab 90, Vantage 85). Damage drags a new `RaycastVehicle.power_modifier` down: full power above ~half hull, sputtering toward 35% below it, **zero at dead** — brakes and steering untouched. Sustained fire past dead (−35) kills the driver: same card, same $300.
- **The getaway loop is the point:** shot-dead ride → bail on foot → jack something (D-009). The systems were built in this order deliberately.
- **Repair:** the impound pad doubles as the body shop ($1.50/hull point, stopped for 1.6 s); County General restores the current ride and your truck with the $300 bill.
- **One register for gunfire:** cop muzzles scatter pedestrian crowds through the same panic path as the player's pistol.
**Rationale:** since M5 the arms race was one-sided — the player had a pistol, the cops had bumpers, and heat was a taxi meter rather than a threat. The two-tier ladder keeps the game's "dumb fun cops" ethos at low heat while making three stars genuinely dangerous, and hull-not-health keeps M-rated violence pointed at *machines* first (the driver only dies by refusing two explicit outs: the sputter warning and the bail-out banner).
**Verification:** 16-check headless harness (ladder gating at heat 1/2/3 on ride and on foot, sputter curve, engine death, driver death, respawn repair + heat clear, body shop billing, ped binding), then both CI gates green. The harness also surfaced a latent M4 bug now fixed: sky_weather's cached group refs used `x is Type` *before* `is_instance_valid(x)`, which is itself a script error on a freed instance — any cruiser freed between its 0.4 s cache rescans during a storm could throw.
**Owner:** Game Systems Designer + Technical Director. **Status: ADOPTED** — Milad may veto, particularly the ladder thresholds and damage numbers (all JSON/const tunables).

## D-013 · 2026-08-03 · Real geometry, rigged characters, accurate streets
**Decision:** Fidelity pass on all three fronts Milad named. Adopted:
- **A mesh kit** (`scripts/world/mesh_kit.gd`): flat-shaded ArrayMesh builders — `taper` (a box with an independently sized/shifted top face: wedges, tumblehome, truncated pyramids), `loft` (a 2D side profile extruded across a width that narrows with height), `prism`. - **Characters are rigged and animated.** Jointed hips/knees/shoulders/elbows under a bobbing torso; `character_factory.animate()` drives a procedural gait whose **phase advances with distance travelled**, so stride matches ground speed at any pace and feet never skate. Knees bend one way only; arms counter-swing; lean scales with speed; `aim_pose()` overrides with a shooter stance. Driven from player_character (real velocity) and pedestrians (scripted lane/panic speed). Downed peds keep the rig frozen and tumble as physics.
- **Street accuracy:** mast-arm traffic signals with emissive lenses (phase baked per intersection — a live signal system has to negotiate with traffic.gd, deferred), solid lane edge lines, stop bars, turn arrows, retail podiums with sidewalk canopies, rooftop water tanks, hydrants, bins, suburban utility poles.

## D-014 · 2026-08-03 · Shooting and camera mechanics
**Decision:** Combat and camera get a proper feel layer. Adopted:
- **Accuracy is a model, not a constant.** Spread is a cone half-angle from stance (aimed 0.004 rad / hip 0.055) + movement penalty + per-shot bloom that recovers over time. **The crosshair ring is rendered from that same number** — the HUD cannot lie about the gun.
- **Recoil moves the CAMERA, not a cosmetic overlay.** Shots are cast from the camera, so muzzle climb genuinely spoils the follow-up. Recoil is applied to the camera basis only; `orbit_yaw` (the character's movement basis) is deliberately untouched so the player never drifts sideways while shooting.
- **Feedback:** hitmarker (orange and longer on a knockdown or a crippled cruiser), ejected brass on a pooled ballistic arc, camera shake per shot.
- **Damage model:** head hits ×3 (a hit above the body's head plane), falloff from 45 m to a 35% floor at 130 m.
- **Drive-by** from the driver's seat: slower cadence, much wider cone, no aim mode, and a forward arc block so you cannot shoot through your own hood.
- **Camera spring arm** on both rigs — the lens stops clipping into buildings. Pulls in instantly (a frame inside geometry is a frame of black), eases back out. `hit_from_inside` is on, so a pivot already buried in a wall still reports.
- **Trauma-based shake** with a single `add_trauma()` entry point, fed by firing, taking rounds, and crashes (reusing vehicle_audio's existing impact energy — one source of truth for "how hard did we hit").
- **Mouse is now captured while driving**, enabling free-look (springs back after 0.9 s) and drive-by aiming; ESC releases in both modes.
**Rationale:** "can we improve shooting and camera mechanics" (Milad, 2026-08-03). The pistol was pinpoint-accurate with zero feedback and the camera happily sat inside towers — the two things that most separate a prototype from a game that feels good.
**Verification:** a 16-check headless harness asserting the mechanics numerically (aiming tighter than hip, sprint worse than standing, bloom widening, scatter provably inside the cone and non-degenerate, falloff curve endpoints, head hits > body hits > distant hits, trauma clamping, and the spring arm stopping 13.6 m short of a 65 m ray through Cattleman's Trust while still extending fully in the open). One harness bug found and fixed — the first spring-arm test aimed down an empty street because it used the grid centre rather than the tower's real coordinates. Both gates green, smoke baseline byte-identical.
**Owner:** Game Systems Designer + Rendering Engineer. **Status: ADOPTED** — every constant is at the top of `combat.gd` / `chase_camera.gd`; mouse-capture-while-driving is the most likely thing Milad may want reverted.

**D-016 addendum (same day) — the REAL reason Milad "couldn't aim," found on his second report ("I still cant aim, move camera in pedestrian mode"):** on-foot mouse look had been dead in live play the whole time. ## D-020 · 2026-08-10 · The joint sign law (elbows have been backwards since M10)
**Decision:** Milad, watching the M16 crowd walk: *"whn ppl walk the elbows are backwards."* He was right, and the bug was five milestones old.
**Root cause:** a limb in this rig hangs down local −Y, so a **positive** `rotation.x` swings it toward −Z = **forward**. Knee flexion is backward (heel to butt) → negative, which M10 got right. **Elbow flexion is forward** → must be positive. M10 copied the knee's sign to the elbow. Knees and elbows are mirror joints; they can never share a sign. The same inverted convention then propagated to `aim_pose` (the gun arm swung *behind* the body) and, in M16, into `melee.gd` (every punch was thrown backwards — the melee agent even re-pitched the bat prop to 2.60 rad to compensate for an arm pointing the wrong way, which is the tell).
**Fixed:** `character_factory.animate()` elbow → positive; `aim_pose()` shoulders/elbows → positive; `melee.gd` SH_COCK/SH_PUNCH/EL_* signs corrected (cock pulls back = negative, punch drives forward = positive, guard raises forearms in front); `BAT_PITCH` re-derived from 2.60 to −0.55 now that it no longer compensates for a reversed arm.
**Why five harnesses missed it:** every prior animation check asserted **joint angles** (`knee.rotation.x < 0`, "hip swing range"), which a sign error satisfies happily. The new check measures **where the hand actually ends up in the body's own space** — a flexed elbow must place the hand at a smaller local z than the elbow; the aimed gun hand must be ≥0.20 m in front of the shoulder and above 1.15 m. 7/7, harness deleted. **Standing rule: pose verification asserts POSITIONS, not angles.**
**Also (same session):** the M16 upper body measured **0.63 m across the chest** including arms (a human is ~0.50 m) — that width, not the head size, is what made heads look small and torsos read as slabs. SHOULDER_X 0.215→0.183, deltoid/yoke/thorax/pec/scapula narrowed to match: chest span 0.63→0.54 m. Measured, not eyeballed.
**Honest remaining gap:** faces still do not hold at a true close-up (eyes read as dark blobs under the brow, and some wardrobe panels — pockets, plackets — float visibly off the shirt). Logged as the next character pass. New judgement vantages `face`/`torso`/`side`/`back` exist in `zz_shot.gd` so it is judged at the distance Milad actually screenshots.

## D-025 · 2026-08-11 · A LATENT PHYSICS BUG — every suspension force swings on the wrong lever
**Found by the Car Designer in QA cycle 2, deriving a per-axle ride-height solve. NOT FIXED — deliberately.**
`raycast_vehicle.gd` applies suspension and tyre forces as:
```gdscript
apply_force(up * spring_force, hard_global - com_global)
```
Godot's `position` argument is an offset from the body **origin**, and the engine subtracts the centre of mass itself. Passing an offset that already has the CoM removed therefore subtracts it **twice** — every suspension and tyre force in the vehicle has been swinging on `hard − 2·com` since M1.
**Evidence (predicted front-axle load share, single vs double lever, against the settled car — five for five on the doubled lever):** Vantage 0.6138 / 0.7172 vs measured **0.7175**; Interceptor 0.4885 / 0.4689 vs **0.4689**; Brisket 0.5778 / 0.6333 vs **0.6335**; Wrecker 0.5676 / 0.6081 vs **0.6081**; Slab 0.5434 / 0.5723 vs **0.5722**.
**Why it was not fixed, and why that was right:** correcting it changes the Longhorn Wrecker's motion, which breaks the byte-identical smoke baseline this project has held for eleven milestones, and it invalidates **every tuning number in every profile** — mass, springs, damping, CoM, grip were all tuned against the doubled lever, so they are all compensating for it. This is an architectural decision for the **Physics Engineer + Technical Director**, not a defect for an art agent to patch. The body builder now mirrors the solver *as it is*, with a comment requiring the factor of 2 to be removed in the same edit if `force_pos` is ever corrected.
**The decision that is owed:** either (a) leave it — the cars feel how they feel, the numbers are self-consistent, and "wrong lever, right result" is a legitimate place to stop for a stylised game; or (b) correct it and re-tune the whole fleet from scratch against a new smoke baseline, with a documented baseline change. **Recommendation: (a) for now, revisit only if a handling feature (weight transfer, load-sensitive grip, trailer physics) needs the lever to be true.**
**Also found in the same pass, and fixed:** the builder was drawing arches at a ride height the car never sits at (an even quarter-mass split rather than per-axle) — wheels missed their own arches by ±37.6 mm on the Vantage and ±42.3 on the Brisket, on a bar row 45 mm wide; and the Slab's **whitewall band was wound inside-out** (`n.x = −0.994`), so back-face culling had deleted the car's one piece of period jewellery from every angle a player can stand in.

## D-027 · 2026-08-11 · The project's first frame-rate baseline — and what it took to get an honest one
**Owner:** Rendering Engineer. **Status: ADOPTED.**
The quality bar has carried a "60 fps at street level downtown" row since cycle 1 and it had **never once been run**. Every performance claim in the project — including my own "120–140 fps" for the suburb lights — was an opinion with a number stapled to it. `game/scripts/systems/perf_harness.gd` replaces all of them. Ten stations (downtown / suburb / freeway / hospital / **the post-death respawn frame**, day and night), one command, one table, left in the tree as infrastructure. Manual: `docs/tech/rendering/perf-harness.md`.
**The result: every station holds 60 fps at p95 on the dev machine, and boot-to-playable is 3.5 s against a 20 s bar.** Both §4b rows pass, with numbers, for the first time.
**Three things had to be got right before the numbers meant anything, and each was a trap:**
- **The player has to be teleported.** `traffic.gd` spawns in a ring around the *player vehicle* and `pedestrians.gd` anchors on the player actor. A free camera parked in the suburb while the truck sits downtown measures an empty suburb — a beautiful number and a false one. Each station now moves the rig onto real pavement and prints where it landed and what population it achieved.
- **The GPU clock does not exist here.** Godot 4.7.1's Metal backend returns 0.00 ms from `viewport_get_measured_render_time_gpu` every frame. The harness detects the all-zero column and prints `n/a` rather than a zero somebody reads as "free".

**County General was lit (D-069).** Fixing the suburb did not make the hospital darker, it made it *first*: mean 4.23/255 with 92.3% of the ground half under 8/255, at the **mandatory post-death respawn**. ## D-028 · 2026-08-11 · The shadow policy — `cast_shadow` is now a decision every MultiMesh has to make out loud
**Owner:** Rendering Engineer. **Status: ADOPTED** (QA verifies; I may not close my own defect).
D-027 measured the win and filed it. This cashes it. A MultiMesh is frustum-culled as **one unit**, so a single visible instance drags the whole set into every directional-shadow cascade, every frame — and the audit found **189 MultiMeshes, 63,563 instances, 55,251 of them (87%) in every cascade**, led by 11,919 grass tufts, 3,968 bluestem, 3,255 fence pickets and 5,490 road-paint quads. Only 5 of 13 MultiMesh-building files set the flag at all.
**After this pass: 10,579 instances (17%) cast. 44,672 instances and 85 of the 189 sets stopped occluding the sun.** Geometry, colliders, seeded draw order and instance counts are byte-identical — the audit reports the same 189 / 63,563 before and after. This was a render-flag pass and nothing else.

**The rule, and it is now enforced by the type system, not by discipline.** `casts` (or `shadows`) is a **required parameter with no default** on the MultiMesh builder in every one of the nine files. A layer added next milestone cannot inherit a shadow policy by forgetting to think about one; it will not compile until somebody answers. `scenic_dressing.gd` is the proof this matters: it was one of the five files that already *had* the flag, and it still shipped 6,142 casting instances of two-sided grass cards — because the flag had `= true` as a default. The default is gone.
- **Flat on the ground casts nothing** — paint, lane dashes, crosswalks, curb paint, tyre wear, asphalt scars, manholes (20 mm), storm grates (30 mm), driveways (40 mm), caliche aprons (30 mm), suburb pavement slabs (60 mm), parking-stall lines, curb ramps and their 20 mm tactile pads.
- **Thin scatter and wire casts nothing** — grass tufts, bluestem, yucca, prickly pear (all *two-sided card* geometry: the shadow map has no alpha and no thickness, so a caster renders a hard black shard), tumbleweeds, fence wire, power lines (45 mm at 8.5 m), delineator posts, and the suburb's post-and-rail fence (0.12 m posts, 0.05 × 0.10 m rails — 3,255 instances of comb-shaped shimmer).
- **Relief bolted to a wall that already casts** — storefront pilasters, bulkheads, fascias, doors, glazing, house trim, garage doors, window panes, sign plates hung on a mast, grime streaks, fly-posters. These can only shade the surface they are glued to; that is shadow acne, not shading.
- **Emissive fixtures cast nothing.** A lamp head, a signal lens, a porch light or a lit shop window has no business being an occluder in the sun's cascade. The signal lenses were the sharpest case: unlit lenses are collapsed to a **zero-scale basis**, so casting pushed 1,176 degenerate instances through every cascade to draw nothing.
- **Real volumes keep their shadows** — buildings, podiums, towers, columns, poles, masts, signal housings, benches, planters, meters, news boxes, bus shelters, dumpsters, sheds, chimneys, roofs, trampolines, bike racks, plaza walls, flagpoles, bollards, porta-potties, debris, landmark massing, and **every tree in the game**.

**Three sets kept their shadows that the rule of thumb would have killed, each for a stated reason.** *Storefront awnings* (385) — 1.35 m of overhang at 3.3 m, and the band of shade it lays on the sidewalk is what makes a shopfront read as sheltered rather than painted on. *Rooftop AC units* (159) — free-standing mass on an open podium deck; without their shadows the roof is a flat gray lid from every tower window and from `aerial`. *Stonebridle stop signs* (156) — a 0.84 m octagon at driver eye height, the only sign in the suburb big enough to lay a readable disc on pale caliche; the 70 mm street-name blades above it went off. *Plaza curb stops* (224) also stayed: 224 concrete blocks loose on an empty apron read as more paint without the strip of shade under each one.
**One judgement call worth flagging for QA: freeway guardrails (842 instances) went OFF.** They are a 0.15 m beam and 0.10 m post at the **deck edge** — overhead sun hides the shadow under the rail, low sun throws it off the side of the deck into open air. `freeway_deck` before/after is pixel-indistinguishable. If QA disagrees, it is one word in `freeway_dressing.gd`.

**THE MEASUREMENT, and the method it forced.** The dev machine drifted **40% between runs** during this cycle — an identical build measured 8.33 ms and 14.29 ms at `downtown_day` twenty minutes apart — so a sequential before/after was worthless and said every station got *slower* while its draw calls went *down*. ## D-030 · 2026-08-11 · The project's first surface shaders (one → five)
**Decision:** Adopted. Milad: *"advanced graphics… right now it feels like an arcade game."* Diagnosis: every surface in the game was a stock `StandardMaterial3D` with a procedural albedo and a roughness number. The tree had exactly **one** custom shader — the sky, and it is the best-looking thing in the game. That was the gap.
**Shipped:** road/asphalt+concrete, wet response, building glass, car paint+glass, skin.
- **The road pays for itself.** Godot's triplanar path samples every texture **three times**; projecting flat on world XZ needs **two** — so micro-normal, macro variation, wheel tracks and rain all fit inside a net sample *reduction*, across ~566,000 m² on one shared material. Wheel-polished lanes are **derived analytically** from the city's own lattice (`BLOCK 60 + STREET 26 = 86`), so at an intersection both axes fire and the tracks cross — which is what an intersection actually looks like.
- **Wetness is a quantity with memory** (14 s soak, 105 s dry), deliberately NOT the same signal as grip, which is a flag. Grip has dropped to 0.72 in rain since M16 **while the pavement rendered bone dry**; now the street stays dark and mirrored well after the rain stops.

**Perf, interleaved on one tree** (every builder forks on `--gfx-legacy` returning the *original* function, so the control arm IS the old look, not a reimagination of it): **cost-neutral to ~4% best-case, ~7% median on the heaviest station.** Not the free win the sample math implied — the ALU eats the saved sample. ## D-031 · 2026-08-11 · ADOPT the skinned character architecture, in stages
**Decision:** **ADOPTED.** Milad: *"Character design is still super poor… character designers have been doing a shit job."* They were not. They were working against a Milestone-1 architecture ceiling, and a Technical Artist was brought in with a different lens to test whether the ceiling was real. It is, and the replacement is measurably better **and faster**.
**The mechanism, stated more precisely than I did:** it is not merely that rigid parts cannot deform — **there is no fix**. Two smooth convex surfaces that cross are tangential *somewhere* along the crossing curve; that is geometry, not craftsmanship. Every cycle removes one crossing and the next becomes the loudest, which is exactly the history of D-002 (stipple), D-024 (muzzle) and D-027 (crease). A blended union has **no crossing curve at all** — the defect class becomes unrepresentable rather than merely smaller.
**Measured (proof-of-concept, `--skinned`, inert by default):**
- **Visible seams between rigid parts, counted by per-part id buffers: −77% overall** (58,285 → 13,626 owner-change edges across 8 vantages). At `elbow`, the one vantage with no head in frame: **9,873 → 188, and 53 visible parts → 2.** Essentially all residual is the head, deliberately left as factory code.
- **The finding that explains the whole history:** through a gait cycle the factory's shoulder seam count swings **10.7%** — the parts slide across each other, so *which* crossing is exposed depends on the animation phase. That is why every cycle found a new one. The skinned shoulder is constant to **1.1%**: there is nothing to expose.
- **Faster, not slower.** Draw calls down (3,749 → 3,499 downtown), body meshes per character 83 → **1**, materials ~20 → **1**. **`hospital_door_day` — the post-death respawn frame, the one view no player can avoid — went from `p95 16.98 ms / OVER BUDGET` to `p95 12.50 ms`, every station holding 60 fps, in both interleaved rounds.** LOD0 triangles more than double and frame time still falls, because 20k triangles in one draw beats 8k in 83 draws — and because the rigid build **cannot have** an LOD chain at all (decimating a hierarchy of separate meshes pulls the parts apart).
- The rig contract holds: `build()` is the only symbol that changes at a call site, `animate()`/`aim_pose()` are **delegated to the factory's own functions verbatim**, and D-020 was verified by position (`sh_1.rotation.x = 1.45` moves bone `el_1` to z = −0.2978, forward).

## D-033 · 2026-08-11 · The beacon veil is FIXED, and the mechanism was the blend mode
**Decision:** Adopted. `beacon_kit` now carries four rules; all five callers re-tuned. D-032 identified the object; this entry records *why* it veiled and what replaced it.
**The mechanism — it was never a beam of light. It was a pane of smoked orange glass.** `column_material()` set `TRANSPARENCY_ALPHA`, whose default blend is **MIX**: `out = src·a + dst·(1−a)`. Every covered pixel was 34% *replaced* by orange **and** the background it replaced was crushed to 66%. That is a veil in the literal photographic sense, and it is the complete explanation for three cycles of mis-diagnosis: **it repaints whatever stands behind it, so every cycle blamed the thing being repainted.**
**The decisive proof is one sign.** Across a frozen-world differential render of all 59 vantages the veil measured dR +14…+38 / dG −1…+8 / **dB −7…−1 — the blue channel goes DOWN in 12 of 14 touched vantages. An additive light source cannot subtract blue.** That single fact settles it without argument. Worse: in **daylight the median luminance change over the covered pixels was −0.5 to +0.7.** At noon the old beacon added no light at all. It only re-tinted.
**Three compounding failures behind the one blend bug:**
- **No distance term.** Per-pixel opacity was constant while screen coverage grows as 1/d², so damage peaked exactly where a marker is least useful. From 11 m it repainted **80,693 px (5.6% of frame; 110,920 at night)**; from the impound pad at 371 m — the range it exists for — it reached **25 px and was not findable in daylight. The budget was spent backwards.**
- `pow(v, 1.75)` left ~0.10 alpha at half height over a 21 m shaft — QA's "soft salmon column over the sky."
- **The corona had never worked on 7 of 9 junker slots.** They stand on a lot slab whose top is y=0.2; the beacon was pinned to y=0 and the corona to +0.062, so it sat **138 mm under the pavement and rendered zero pixels.** Four cycles missed it *because the broken column was loud enough to hide that half the beacon was missing.*
**The four rules now in the kit:** a beacon never subtracts light (**`BLEND_MODE_ADD`**); the column is a **long-range instrument**, driven by a `Driver` node from camera distance to the beacon's *foot* (Godot's built-in distance fade is per-fragment and fades a tall shaft's **bottom** while leaving its top lit — backwards); the **corona carries close range**; and **it stays depth-tested** — a shaft drawing over the tower in front of it *is* a screen-space veil by definition and would re-open this defect on day one. The see-through job already belongs to the HUD, which prints a compass arrow and a live metre count no building can hide.
**Measured (frozen world, beacon-on minus beacon-hidden, drift-masked, frustum-gated; non-beacon drift 8 px across 59 vantages):** marker columns **12 → 3**; **in the 12 judgement vantages 5 → 0**; total repainted pixels **285,922 → 24,884 (−91%)**; vantages where a beacon *subtracts* light **12 → 0**; `car_side` frame height 45.2% → 4.2%, `hero_pickup` 45.8% → 3.0%, `face` 25.6% → 2.6%. The three surviving columns are intentional and named.
**Findability inverted rather than neutered:** 9/14 vantages before, **9/14 after** — but median daylight luminance lift went from ≈0-or-negative at *every* daytime vantage to positive at all of them (downtown 157 m −0.5 → **+6.6**; on foot 11 m +3.2 → **+17.2**; driving 25 m +0.7 → **+15.7**). Impound pad 371 m peak lift 30.5 → **59.8** day, 103.6 → **149.2** night.
**Visual confirmation of the D-032 chain:** at 11 m the notorious **"orange tree canopy" is green again in both lobes, with zero changes to any tree code.**
**Also fixed:** `repo_board._arrow()` guarded `length_squared() < 0.001`, which is **false for NaN**, so a non-finite basis reached `normalize()`. A HUD that renders a direction it does not have is a HUD that lies.
**Kept as permanent QA infrastructure:** `zz_beacon_probe.gd` — `--nobeacon` hides the whole `objective_beacon` group so any future cycle can **subtract two frames instead of arguing about what a colour means.** Inert without the flag.
**An honest limit it recorded rather than buried:** the daylight read at 370 m is bounded by **ACES roll-off, not energy** — a 40% energy increase bought 8% more lift. The next lever is *area or motion*, not intensity.
**Gates:** `--smoke` ×2 byte-identical; 6 boots of `--quit-after 900 -- 2>&1`, zero error lines.
**Owner:** Game Systems Designer. **Status: ADOPTED.**

## D-065 · 2026-09-13 · The run is a run: stride scales with speed, the knee flexes in swing, the body sits on its supporting leg — and the femur angles in
**Status: LANDED, gated (quick gate per `docs/qa/evidence/gait-sept13/`); QA to rule on D-138/D-139.**
**Milad:** *"The running looks retarded and the character's proportions and body still looks like shit. Make it look like an actual person. Incorporate any skills to make us get to a better version quicker."*
**The instrument first.** One frame of a gait says nothing about a gait, and `character_review.gd`'s `gait` view was one frame at 3 m/s. It now saves GAIT STRIPS: eight frames across one full stride at phase marks of TAU/8, side-on and from the front quarter, at 1.4 / 3.2 / 6.5 m/s — the pose driven by hand, frozen while each frame renders. The before-strip (`before/sprint_side.png`) made the diagnosis in one look: the sprint was a walk with a lean.
**Three faults in `animate()`, all arithmetic:** (1) the stride was a fixed 1.74 m at every speed, so a 6.5 m/s sprint churned 7.5 steps a second; (2) every joint chased its target through a 12/s exponential blend, which at that cadence (3.75 Hz) passes 45 % of the amplitude and lags it 45 degrees — the legs never opened; (3) the knee's flexion window (`-sin(phase + 0.9)`) sat 140 degrees from where swing is: the knee bent in STANCE and the leg swung forward straight — a goose step. **Now:** stride = 0.75 + 0.5·speed m per cycle (1.9 / 2.7 / 3.3 steps a second); gait joints blend at 40/s (idle keeps 12/s); knee flexion peaks in mid-swing (`cos(ph + 0.35)`, heel toward the seat, 1.05 rad walking, 1.95 sprinting) with a mid-stance loading bend (0.30 / 0.65) and, at a run, a landing bend so the foot comes down under the body rather than reaching; the thigh's mean is biased forward at a run (+46/−25 degrees at a sprint); elbows at ~90 degrees pumping at ±0.6 rad from the shoulder, closing on the front swing and opening on the back; torso counter-rotation 0.12 rad and a 10-degree lean. **Height:** a walker's body sits on its supporting leg's geometry every frame (thigh·cos + shin·cos, the longer leg), so the foot is on the ground at every phase — it floated 5 cm at double support before; a runner blends to a designed curve, 5 cm low at mid-stance and 4 cm up into flight.
**The body (`_prims`, cache v24):** the knees moved 28 mm inboard of the hips (`KNEE_IN`, the knee bones with them; 20 mm measured but did not read at 350 px/m, 35 mm welded the calves at 18 mm) — the hip joints are 0.20 m apart on this rig and the knees sat straight below them, then the leg was abducted a further 2.6 degrees: an A-frame in every front plate. The femur angles in, the shin is vertical, only the feet toe out; `LEG_ABDUCT` 0.045 → 0.02. The deltoid's widest point moved 2.5 cm below the acromion (centre 1.418 → 1.395, height scale 1.18 → 1.06): it had been widest AT the corner, a shoulder pad. The knee is r52 (was 62: it measured thicker than the calf), the deltoid tapers r50 → r38 into the bicep with its z pinched to 0.88 (a ball, not a cylinder), the lats sit inboard of the rib wall and the pecs are a touch wider. Measured: knee 0.108 (target 0.11), knee outer span 0.252 (centres ±0.072, were ±0.106), ankles under the knees, shoulders 0.468, trunk unchanged, the arm clear of the trunk by one cell at the armpit floor.
**"Incorporate any skills":** no installed skill covers anatomy or gait (they are video and marketing skills), so the guild was used as it was meant to be — the Character Designer and the Movement Mechanics Engineer reviewed the first pass's plates read-only (Sonnet, ~7 minutes each, no Godot) and wrote ranked lists with the exact primitive or constant to change; the top four from each landed in the same round (`docs/qa/evidence/gait-sept13/review_*.md`). The review stage also gained ambient occlusion, as the game has, so a white sleeve on a white shirt can show a crease.
**Owner:** producer. **Open:** the armpit renders as a one-cell slot, not a crease (the field is right; a sleeve shell covers it); cloth volume — the shirt and trousers are paint (D-060, the shell wardrobe milestone: sleeve shell first, then a torso shell with a gathering hem, then trouser volume); no ankle bone (the foot follows the shin); no pelvis bone (the hips do not counter-rotate); the hands hang with the fingers straight.
