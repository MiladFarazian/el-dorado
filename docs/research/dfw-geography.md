# DFW Geography — Canonical World Reference

Research snapshot: late July 2026. Real names throughout; every place will receive a parody rename in-game (noted as "parody hook" where a strong angle exists). Each entry carries a **game note** — why it earns space on an open-world map.

---

## 1. Macro Structure: The Dumbbell Metro

DFW is not one city. It is a **dumbbell**: two genuine downtowns ~32 miles apart, connected by a bar of suburbs, with a giant airport sitting between them like a third city. ~8.4M people, ~9,200 sq mi — the fastest-growing large metro in the US. No geographic constraint (no ocean, no mountains) means growth radiates in all directions, hardest to the north.

| Zone | Real identity | Game note |
|---|---|---|
| **Downtown Dallas** | Glass/LED skyline, banking + law, freeway noose (I-30/I-35E/I-345/Woodall Rodgers) around the core | The "money" skyline; night-lit towers are the map's primary orientation beacon from 20+ miles |
| **Downtown Fort Worth** | Smaller, brick + Art Deco, walkable Sundance Square, cowtown self-image | Second full city center = rare in games; different police, different radio, different vibe per side of map |
| **Arlington + Mid-Cities** | The bar of the dumbbell: Arlington's stadium district, then Grand Prairie, Irving, Euless/Bedford/Hurst/Grapevine | Stadium/theme-park playground midway; Mid-Cities residential filler is the top candidate for compression |
| **DFW International Airport** | 27 sq mi — larger than Manhattan — its own city (police, ZIP, hotels); N-S spine highway between the terminals | A landmass, not a building: restricted-zone gameplay, cargo heists, five terminals + Terminal F rising (2027) |
| **Northern boom corridor** | Plano → Frisco → McKinney → Prosper/Celina along US-75 + Dallas North Tollway; corporate HQ row, $2M tract mansions, churn of bulldozed prairie | The satire goldmine: HQ campuses, megachurches, master-planned "towns," construction frontier that visibly eats farmland |
| **Southern Dallas / Oak Cliff** | South of the Trinity: historically redlined, Black and Latino neighborhoods, Bishop Arts gentrification frontier, Great Trinity Forest, escarpment hills | The map's soul and its wilderness; most vertical terrain in the metro; where story grounding lives |
| **Western edge (Cross Timbers)** | Past Loop 820: oak scrub ridges, Lockheed's F-35 plant, Eagle Mountain and Benbrook lakes, then ranchland | Natural map edge: the west can simply fade into cedar-and-oak nothing, the way GTA V fades into desert |

**The core layout truth:** Dallas faces east/north (prairie, finance, sprawl); Fort Worth faces west (cattle, defense, ranches). The game map should read as a gradient from glass (east) to brick and dust (west).

## 2. Terrain & Nature

Flat is the default — but not featureless. Get these five terrain systems right and the map feels like Texas:

- **Blackland Prairie.** Deep black clay flatness across Dallas and the whole northern corridor. Horizon-wide sightlines; the skyline is visible from 20 miles. Clay soil cracks foundations and buckles roads (real Texans joke about it). **Game note:** flatness makes tall things sacred — every tower, water tower, and radio mast is a landmark; use them as navigation instead of terrain.
- **White Rock Escarpment.** Austin Chalk limestone ridge running through Oak Cliff, Duncanville, Cedar Hill, Midlothian — 100–200 ft of genuine relief, the metro's only "hills." Cedar Hill State Park + the **Cedar Hill antenna farm** (a forest of 1,500-ft TV masts with blinking red lights) sit on the high point. **Game note:** the one place for canyon roads, overlooks of both skylines, and a mountain-radio-tower setpiece; I-20 cuts through it in a visible limestone notch.
- **Trinity River system.** Four forks (West + Clear Fork meet in Fort Worth; Elm Fork joins the West Fork near downtown Dallas; East Fork out by Lake Ray Hubbard). Through Dallas the river runs inside a mile-wide **leveed floodway** — a vast, empty green channel with the skyline behind it, crossed by the Margaret Hunt Hill and McDermott arch bridges. Downstream: the **Great Trinity Forest**, ~6,000 acres, the largest bottomland hardwood urban forest in the US, with feral hogs, gar, and dumped cars. **Game note:** the floodway is a ready-made illegal-race/chase arena (real street racers use it); the Forest is a swamp-wilderness biome ten minutes from downtown — body-dump missions write themselves.
- **Lakes — all fake.** Every lake is a dammed reservoir (Texas has ~no natural lakes). Key ring: **Grapevine** (wooded, next to the airport), **Lewisville** (biggest, north), **Ray Hubbard** (east, crossed by the I-30 bridge), **White Rock** (1911, urban jewel with Arboretum + sailing), **Joe Pool** (southwest, next to the escarpment), **Benbrook** (SW Fort Worth), **Eagle Mountain + Lake Worth** (NW Fort Worth, stilt houses and boat bars). **Game note:** lakes are the map's water gameplay AND its natural edges; dams, marinas, party coves, and drought-exposed lakebeds (dropped 20+ ft in bad summers) are all content.
- **Cross Timbers.** Two ancient north-south belts of gnarled post-oak on sandy soil — the Eastern belt threads between Dallas and Fort Worth (Grapevine/Flower Mound), the Western belt begins west of Fort Worth. Historically the frontier line between farm country and ranch country. **Game note:** free biome variety on an otherwise flat map; the western belt is the map's wild edge.

**Sky is terrain here.** With no mountains, the sky does the topography: 40,000-ft supercell anvils, wall clouds, shelf clouds rolling in like a wave, and sunsets that turn the entire western sky orange. Budget accordingly (see §6).

## 3. The Road Network AS Gameplay

DFW is arguably the most freeway-defined metro in America. The network is the game's circulatory system and its personality.

| Road | Character | Game note |
|---|---|---|
| **I-30** | The dumbbell bar: downtown Dallas ↔ Arlington stadiums ↔ downtown Fort Worth; crosses Lake Ray Hubbard east | The spine drive of the whole game; both skylines bookend it at night |
| **I-35E (Stemmons)** | Dallas's north-south trunk past the Design District, Medical District, Love Field edge | Warehouse/motel corridor = mission country; chronically jammed, so chases feel plausible |
| **I-35W** | Fort Worth's trunk: downtown → Alliance boom to the north, stockyards edge | The truck-and-logistics freeway; north of downtown it's a wall of distribution centers |
| **I-20** | Southern crosstown linking Fort Worth ↔ Arlington ↔ southern Dallas; cuts the escarpment at Cedar Hill | The "get across the bottom of the map fast" road; big-rig heist territory |
| **I-635 (LBJ)** | Dallas's northern loop; the rebuilt western section runs express toll lanes **in a trench below the free lanes** ("the Canyon") | Double-decker freeway canyon = signature chase geometry; East LBJ is eternal construction |
| **US-75 (Central Expwy)** | North out of downtown, sunk below grade through Uptown/Park Cities, then the Richardson **Telecom Corridor** to Plano/McKinney | A walled expressway trench with cross-bridges — natural jump/roadblock geometry |
| **Dallas North Tollway** | The wealth artery: Uptown → Galleria → Plano's Legacy → Frisco → Prosper; ends where the boom frontier ends | Literal money gradient as you drive north; toll gantries as a mechanic (fines/wanted flags for blowing through) |
| **PGBT (George Bush)** | Outer toll arc through Irving–Carrollton–Richardson–Garland | The "beltway" for the northern half; anonymous, fast, perfect getaway ring |
| **Sam Rayburn Tollway (SH-121)** | Diagonal toll through Frisco/The Colony/Grapevine | Connects every northern playground (Legacy, The Star, Grandscape, DFW) |
| **Loop 820 / SH-183 / SH-114** | Fort Worth's ring; Airport Freeway through the Mid-Cities; 114 threads Las Colinas' mirrored towers to Grapevine | 114 through Las Colinas is the most cinematic non-downtown drive in the metro |
| **Chisholm Trail Pkwy** | Toll road southwest out of Fort Worth toward ranch country | The road that leaves civilization — western map exit |
| **I-45 / US-67 / US-287** | Exits to Houston (SE), Cleburne (SW), and the ranch south | Map-edge feeder roads; where the world runs out |

**Signature interchanges (build these as monuments):**
- **The High Five** (US-75 × I-635): five levels, 120 ft tall, ramps like ribbon pasta. The single most game-worthy road object in Texas.
- **The Mixmaster** (I-30 × I-35E at downtown Dallas's SW corner) and Fort Worth's own rebuilt Mixmaster (I-30 × I-35W): tangled stack interchanges framing each skyline.
- **DFW Connector** (SH-114/121 north of the airport in Grapevine): a braided 24-lane funnel.

**Frontage-road culture (do not skip this):** nearly every Texas freeway is lined with one-way access roads, and commerce lives ON them — gas stations, Whataburgers, strip malls, car dealerships, megachurch signs. U-turn "Texas turnarounds" loop under every overpass. **Game note:** frontage roads give freeways a parallel low-speed lane for shootouts, drive-thrus, and cop evasion, and the turnarounds are built-in 180° chase reversals. No other US metro's road grammar looks like this; nail it and the map is instantly "Texas."

**Toll sprawl as satire:** TxTag/NTTA bills-by-mail, ever-multiplying managed lanes with surge pricing ("Lexus lanes"). Parody hook: a tollway authority as an in-game villainous HOA-with-cameras that mails the player fines.

## 6. Climate & Sky: Weather as a Game System

- **Summer furnace.** 40+ days over 100°F is normal; 105–110°F heat domes; heat shimmer off parking lots, brown lawns by August. **Game note:** heat as ambient hostility — mirages on freeways, packed lakes, NPCs hugging shade, "wet" seasons vs. dust.
- **Supercell season (Apr–Jun).** Rotating storms with green-black bases, wall clouds, gorilla hail (baseball+ size totals cars by the thousand — hail-damage lots and "we'll fix your roof" hustlers are a real economy). **Game note:** a hailstorm that actually dents player vehicles, followed by roofing-scam NPCs, is pure DFW.
- **Tornadoes.** Real history: 1957 Oak Cliff, 2019 EF3 straight through North Dallas. Sirens test at noon on the first Wednesday monthly; wail for real when warned. **Game note:** the rarest, biggest weather event — sirens + green sky + debris = a scripted or systemic showstopper; TV-news storm-chaser culture is a parody-ready media ecosystem.
- **Ice storms.** 1–3 times each winter the metro glazes over and shuts down; overpasses (all 4,000 of them) become skating rinks; February 2021's grid failure is civic scar tissue. **Game note:** ice physics event that flips the whole driving model; grid-failure blackout as a story beat and an energy-market satire target.
- **The sunsets.** Flat horizon + big dust + big clouds = enormous orange-purple skies silhouetting the skylines. **Game note:** golden hour is the map's beauty pass; face the key west-looking vistas (I-30 eastbound at the Dallas skyline, escarpment overlooks) into it.

## 7. Scale Reality: Distances, Iconic Drives, What to Cut

**Real numbers (drive-time truth):**

| Trip | Distance | Time (off-peak / rush) |
|---|---|---|
| Downtown Dallas ↔ Downtown Fort Worth (I-30) | 32 mi | 35 min / 60–75 min |
| Downtown Dallas ↔ DFW Airport | 20 mi | 25 min |
| Downtown Dallas ↔ Frisco (The Star) | 28 mi | 30 min / 60 min |
| Downtown Dallas ↔ Cedar Hill overlook | 18 mi | 25 min |
| Downtown FW ↔ Alliance/TMS | 15–20 mi | 20 min |
| Full metro east–west (Forney → Weatherford) | ~75 mi | 1 hr 20 min+ |

The real metroplex is ~50× the playable area of GTA V's Los Santos. A ~1:6 to 1:8 compression keeps the dumbbell readable: **two full downtowns ~6–8 game-miles apart** still feels like a real road trip by open-world standards.

**Iconic drives to preserve at full fidelity:**
1. I-30 between the downtowns at night (skyline to skyline, stadium glow at midpoint).
2. Dallas North Tollway northbound — the money gradient from Uptown to raw prairie.
3. SH-114 through Las Colinas' mirror towers.
4. The High Five + LBJ Canyon sequence.
5. I-20 through the Cedar Hill escarpment cut.
6. Margaret Hunt Hill Bridge over the empty floodway.
7. A frontage-road strip anywhere — the default Texas street.

**Cut or compress ruthlessly:**
- **Mid-Cities interiors** (Euless/Bedford/Hurst/N. Richland Hills): endless indistinct residential — compress to one representative band along SH-183/121.
- **Garland/Mesquite/Grand Prairie interiors**: same treatment; keep Mesquite's rodeo arena and I-30's lake bridge, drop the rest.
- **The eastern approaches** (Rockwall beyond the lake, Forney): end the map at Lake Ray Hubbard — a lake is a perfect edge.
- **Denton and the far north beyond US-380**: fade to frontier-construction, then prairie; don't build Denton.
- **Southeast Dallas County** past the Great Trinity Forest: let the forest and the Trinity bottoms be the edge.
- Keep **one** of each repeated suburb archetype (one boomburb main street, one 80s edge city, one frontier slab-farm) and let radio + signage differentiate.

**What must NOT be cut even though it's "empty":** the Trinity floodway, the Great Trinity Forest, the escarpment, the lake shorelines, and the Alliance logistics ocean — that emptiness is structural identity, not dead space.

## 8. Mid-2026 Ground Truth (verified changes to bake into canon)

- **DART Silver Line open** (Oct 25, 2025), Plano↔DFW — the northern suburbs now have an east-west train.
- **Universal Kids Resort, Frisco: OPEN** (July 1, 2026) — seven kid-IP lands off the Tollway; parody hook: a saccharine mini-park amid the sports-city hype.
- **DFW Terminal F under construction** (modular megastructures crossing the airfield; phase 1 opens 2027; part of a ~$9B expansion).
- **Kay Bailey Hutchison Convention Center**: $3.5B+ demolition/rebuild underway through ~2029 — downtown Dallas's south edge is a mega construction site.
- **Mavericks arena**: shortlisted to downtown vs. the old Valley View mall site on I-635; target open 2031 (they play at AAC until then).
- **I-345**: teardown-to-trench approved; construction expected ~2028+ — Deep Ellum's elevated freeway lid is on borrowed time.
- **Panther Island**: bypass-channel dirt finally moving in 2026; first private tower proposed — Fort Worth's north side is an active mega-project.
- **Goldman Sachs campus** (Victory Park/NorthEnd) opens 2027 — 5,000 bankers; "Y'all Street" satire is current, not dated.
- **Stockyards**: ~$1B expansion in planning; Stockyards Hotel closed for renovation into early 2027.
- **Southfork Ranch**: being subdivided into 89 luxury estate lots around the preserved mansion.
- **Texas Motor Speedway**: still alive — NASCAR tripleheader each May plus festival bookings.
- **Cotton Bowl**: $140M renovation complete; State Fair runs late Sept–mid Oct with clear-bag rules.
