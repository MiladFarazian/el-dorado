# DFW Culture Bible — Canonical Reference

Research current as of July 2026. Every entry ends with a **→ Game:** note (faction / radio / mission / pedestrian use).
Rule reminder: real institutions get parody renames; no real living person appears as a character — satirize the *type*, never the person.

---

## 1. Identity & Rivalry: Two Cities, One Grudge

- **The core split.** Dallas = glass towers, finance, image management, new money aggressively cosplaying as old money. Fort Worth = "Cowtown," proudly unpretentious, "Where the West Begins." The cities are 32 miles apart and psychologically on different continents. **→ Game:** the map's central tension — every faction, radio station, and billboard picks a side.
- **How locals actually talk.** Fort Worth on Dallas: "Dallas is for people from somewhere else," "all hat, no cattle," "plastic." Dallas on Fort Worth: mostly doesn't think about it — which enrages Fort Worth more than any insult. FW natives say "I'm from Fort Worth, *not* Dallas" within 30 seconds. **→ Game:** pedestrian barks and radio call-ins write themselves.
- **Dallas self-image.** Big D, the city that hosted the TV show *Dallas* and internalized it: deals, hair, debt, a skyline lit like a casino (the green-argon Bank of America tower, the flying red Pegasus). "Dallas is a city with an inferiority complex wearing a superiority complex." **→ Game:** downtown district identity — neon horse logo parody is mandatory.
- **Fort Worth self-image.** Runs on the Bass family's restored downtown (Sundance Square), the Stockyards, and genuine cattle/horse money in Parker County. Fiercely polite, secretly wealthy, allergic to flash. Now also the *Yellowstone*-industrial-complex city (a famous rancher-showrunner type HQ'd here films everything nearby). **→ Game:** a prestige-TV cowboy studio faction satirizing Hollywood-goes-Texas.
- **Suburban planets.** *Frisco*: hyper-growth boomtown — sports HQs, a $5B corporate mile, a new Universal kids park, cricket leagues, brand-new "old town." *Arlington*: the in-between city with no downtown but ALL the stadiums (Cowboys, Rangers, Six Flags) and famously no public transit. *Mesquite*: "Rodeo Capital of Texas" east-side working-class, championship rodeo since 1958. *Plano/Southlake*: corporate-relo money. *Grand Prairie/Duncanville/DeSoto ("Best Southwest")*: Black middle-class suburbs with their own civic pride. **→ Game:** each suburb is a distinct biome — HOA-land, stadium wasteland, rodeo exurb — not generic sprawl.
- **The transplant wave.** Californians and Midwesterners arriving by the corporate jet-load; "Don't California My Texas" bumper stickers vs. natives who secretly love the property values. **→ Game:** pedestrian archetype — the confused transplant in a brand-new F-250 who has never towed anything.

## 2. Cowboy & Rodeo Culture

- **Fort Worth Stockyards.** Twice-daily longhorn cattle drive down Exchange Ave for tourists; Cowtown Coliseum rodeo every Fri/Sat night (world's first indoor rodeo, 1918); Mule Alley boutique redevelopment; a $22M hotel remodel and stalled "Phase 2" expansion lawsuit between mega-developer and ex-executive (2025–26). **→ Game:** a whole district — tourist-facing cowboy theater with a real-estate war underneath. Perfect mission arc.
- **Billy Bob's Texas.** "World's Largest Honky-Tonk," ~100,000 sq ft: concert hall, 30+ bar stations, indoor *live bull riding*, pro country acts weekly. **→ Game:** flagship interior venue — concerts, brawls, bull-riding minigame.
- **Cutting horses & the horse economy.** NCHA Futurity every December at Will Rogers Coliseum, million-dollar purses; Weatherford and Aubrey/Pilot Point = "Horse Country USA," stallion breeding fees like hedge-fund carry. **→ Game:** high-society gambling/heist backdrop — steal a $2M stallion.
- **Fort Worth Stock Show & Rodeo.** Every January, 3+ weeks, Dickies Arena; kids sleep in barns with show steers; a champion steer auctions for six figures to corporate bidders. **→ Game:** seasonal world event; livestock-auction money laundering mission.
- **Black cowboy heritage.** Bill Pickett (inventor of bulldogging) honored in the Stockyards; the Bill Pickett Invitational Rodeo tours here; active Black trail-ride clubs in the southern suburbs — horses, line dancing, zydeco and trail-ride playlists; Fort Worth is home of the "Grandmother of Juneteenth." An estimated 1 in 4 historical cowboys was Black — locals know it, tourists don't. **→ Game:** essential faction/NPC texture; a trail-ride crew as beloved neighborhood institution, treated with full respect.
- **Real vs. cosplay.** The line: Parker County ranchers in mud-caked Ariats vs. Southlake finance guys in $900 unstained Stetsons and $2,000 ostrich boots that have never touched dirt. Locals clock boot wear, hat crease, and truck-bed scratches instantly. **→ Game:** clothing system satire — "authenticity" as literal stat; NPCs mock pristine cowboy fits.

## 3. Car Culture (Deep — This Is a Driving Game)

- **Street takeovers.** Intersections seized at 2 a.m. by hundreds of cars; donuts, sliders, passengers hanging out windows, fireworks at cop cars. Dallas runs a dedicated Street Racing Task Force; two new state laws (effective Sept 2025) target organized takeovers; a single 2025 bust: 24 arrests, dozens of cars impounded. Swarms of dirt bikes/e-bikes blocking Buckner Blvd are their own subculture. **→ Game:** core night-activity loop — heat system, spotter culture, cars seized to impound (which you can raid).
- **Slabs & swangas.** Houston-born, fully adopted in Dallas: candy-paint donks on "swangas" (83/84 elbow wire wheels poking feet past the fender), fifth wheel on the trunk, neon underglow, trunk popped with lit message ("TEXAS MADE"). Slab Sundays cruising MLK Blvd near Fair Park. Slow is the point — "comin' down, swangin'." **→ Game:** customization tree (elbows, candy paint, trunk marquees) + a cruise-slow reputation mechanic where speed *loses* respect.
- **Lowriders.** Mexican-American tradition in Oak Cliff and West Dallas: hydraulics, airbrushed murals (Virgen de Guadalupe, lost family members), Jefferson Blvd cruises, car-club plaques in back windows, family picnic culture at shows. **→ Game:** car-club faction with honor code; hop-off contests as minigame.
- **Lifted trucks / "bro-dozers."** Frisco-to-Weatherford spectrum: $90K lifted diesels, wheels-out "Carolina squat" outlaws, rolling coal on Priuses, frontage-road truck meets in strip-mall parking lots until police push everyone one exit down the highway. **→ Game:** suburban truck-meet events that migrate along the frontage roads in real time.
- **Drag racing lineage.** Yello Belly Drag Strip, Grand Prairie — outlaw eighth-mile institution since 1955, cash "grudge racing," no timing lights shown, run-what-ya-brung. Texas Motorplex (Ennis) is the sanctioned NHRA temple that hosts legal "takeover" nights to drain the street scene. **→ Game:** two racing economies — sanctioned track with purses vs. no-rules cash grudge strip; snitch tension between them.
- **The freeway canvas.** High Five interchange (5 levels), the Mixmaster, LBJ (635) express tunnels, tolled "Lexus lanes," DNT vs. I-35 class divide, service roads with U-turn "Texas turnarounds." Everyone names highways, never numbers-with-I: "Central," "Stemmons," "the Tollway," "the George Bush." **→ Game:** map design brief — the interchange stack is a landmark; toll-lane transponder as gameplay (run tolls, collectors come).

## 4. Music

- **Dallas rap NOW (2026).** The scene's national moment: its biggest star is a burly Dallas rapper (Rolling Stone cover, 2026 mixtape *6WA*, Snoop cameo) who crossed over into country collabs (Shaboozey, Jelly Roll, Luke Combs) — "country-trap" is a real Dallas lane now. His "Sixers" orbit (Ro$ama, Yung Hood, Kaine); risers: Hashi Senjoo, Oak Cliff storyteller Scuttino, CMG-signed Zillionaire Doe. **→ Game:** flagship hip-hop station; a country-rap crossover star archetype is pure DFW 2026 satire.
- **Dallas rap legacy.** The D.O.C. (N.W.A's pen, Dallas native), Big Tuck ("Southside Da Realist"), Dorrough ("Ice Cream Paint Job" — a car anthem), Mo3 (beloved street singer murdered on I-35 in 2020; case arrests as late as 2025 — the wound is still open), Trapboy Freddy, Yella Beezy. **→ Game:** the murdered-star mythology (fictionalized) is a whole mission thread; "Ice Cream Paint Job" energy = slab station DNA.
- **D-Town Boogie.** Dallas's 2007–2010 dance-rap wave: "My Dougie," "Stanky Legg," "Ricky Bobby" — invented dances that went national without credit. Locals still boogie at parties. **→ Game:** throwback block-party station; NPC dance animations with real regional specificity.
- **Neo-soul royalty.** Erykah Badu is Dallas's patron saint (South Dallas, still throws her legendary birthday concerts); Grapevine raised a global pop-rap star who pivoted to country and beer-brand Americana. **→ Game:** a Badu-esque *institution* (venue/festival, not person) anchors the soul station.
- **Red dirt / Texas country.** Fort Worth's own outlaw (metalhead-turned-independent-country millionaire) leads a scene of Turnpike Troubadours, Koe Wetzel, Treaty Oak Revival, Wyatt Flores, Cody Johnson — dance-hall circuit, grunge-country guitars, fiercely independent of Nashville. Billy Bob's is the cathedral. **→ Game:** the honky-tonk station; "independent vs. Nashville sellout" is a satire-rich artist storyline.
- **Deep Ellum.** 1920s blues birthplace (Blind Lemon Jefferson, Leadbelly played here) → punk/alt 80s–90s → today ~30 venues (Trees, The Bomb Factory/Factory, Club Dada) fighting a doom loop: violence, weekend curfews, a nightclub shut by the city attorney (2025), construction, closures — "is Deep Ellum dying" is a permanent argument. **→ Game:** the live-music district with a decay/renewal dynamic the player can influence; venue-owner faction vs. city hall.
- **Tejano & norteño.** Jefferson Blvd and Northwest Dallas: conjunto accordions, norteño mega-acts at Fair Park arenas, quinceañera bands, Selena murals and tribute nights everywhere. **→ Game:** Spanish-language station mixing norteño/Tejano/corridos tumbados — Oak Cliff's driving soundtrack.
- **Gospel.** DFW is a gospel-industrial center — megachurch worship-arena productions, historic South Dallas choirs, Sunday-radio institutions. **→ Game:** Sunday-morning gospel station whose ads are all prosperity-gospel parody.

## 5. Sports Religion

- **The Cowboys.** "America's Team" — a $10B+ brand with no NFC title game since 1995. The owner-GM (80s billionaire archetype) is the most satirizable figure in American sport: sells sponsorships on the practice facility, monologues to media daily, and in Aug 2025 traded the team's superstar pass-rusher to Green Bay over a contract feud — fans called themselves "the laughingstock of the sports world" while the star thrived elsewhere. Palaces: AT&T Stadium ("JerryWorld," Arlington, world's largest video board) and The Star in Frisco. **→ Game:** the anchor satire institution — a football empire run as a reality show; owner is an original character, the *institution* is the target.
- **Friday Night Lights.** Texas HS football is the true religion: Allen's $60M, 18,000-seat high-school stadium; Southlake Carroll and Duncanville dynasties; state championships played *at the Cowboys' stadium*; booster-club politics, recruiting scandals, entire towns emptying for road playoff games. **→ Game:** December playoff world-events; a booster-club corruption mission line; radio broadcasts a fictional rivalry all season.
- **Rangers.** Won the 2023 World Series (franchise's first) then slid back to mediocrity — fans got one parade and returned to fatalism. Globe Life Field: a $1.2B roofed park across from the stadium it replaced at 20 years old (Arlington demolishes and rebuilds stadiums like phone upgrades). **→ Game:** Arlington = "Stadiumburg," a district that is literally three arenas, parking oceans, and a Six Flags.
- **Mavericks.** Feb 2025: the franchise traded its 25-year-old global superstar to the Lakers — instantly "the most shocking trade in NBA history." Fans protested outside the arena, chanted for the GM's firing for months (he was gone within a year), then the team lottery-lucked into the #1 pick, a generational rookie who reset the city's mood in 2026. **→ Game:** a whole satire arc — a beloved athlete traded by a delusional exec, fan riots, then luck bails the franchise out. Sports-talk gold.
- **Stars.** Perennial contender that loses the Western Conference Final annually (2023, '24, '25) — fired a successful coach out of frustration. Winter Classic energy, "Dallas, Stars" goal call. **→ Game:** the "always almost" franchise archetype.
- **Sports-talk ecosystem.** The Ticket (1310 AM) is a 30-year cult: hosts with in-jokes, fake characters, listener "P1" identity, annual listener cruise-ship-level events; competitors 105.3 The Fan and ESPN affiliates. Callers argue the Cowboys 12 months a year; games are almost secondary. **→ Game:** the single best radio-parody template in the region — a talk station with bits, fake drops, and P1-style superfans is mandatory.
- **Also true:** the WNBA's Wings (Arlington) landed a superstar guard in 2025; Major League Cricket's Texas franchise packs Grand Prairie Stadium with South Asian families — cricket at scale, in Texas. **→ Game:** cricket-stadium district detail nobody else's game world would have.

## 6. The Faith Economy

- **Megachurch capital.** Prestonwood (Plano, ~45K members), Fellowship Church, The Potter's House (South Dallas — its famous founder-bishop retired/transitioned leadership in 2025), First Baptist Dallas downtown (politically wired), Watermark, Gateway (Southlake). Campuses like malls: coffee bars, bookstores, parking teams with wands, jumbotron preaching, satellite "campuses" via video feed. **→ Game:** a megachurch faction with corporate org-chart, security detail, and branded merch — a core satire pillar.
- **The Gateway collapse (2024–26), the cautionary epic.** Founder confessed decades-old child sexual abuse after a survivor went public; pleaded guilty (2025): 10-year suspended sentence, 6 months county jail, $270K restitution. Church tithes fell 35–40%; then founder and church fought publicly *over his multimillion-dollar retirement payout* (settled to arbitration, May 2026), plus ongoing defamation and fraud suits. **→ Game:** fictionalized: an empire-church imploding while leadership fights over the money — mission arc, radio-news serial, protest peds outside. Handle as institutional satire only.
- **Prosperity gospel.** Private jets defended on TV ("demons on commercial flights"), seed-faith giving ("plant a $1,000 seed"), sermon series with movie-trailer marketing, book-launch pyramid energy. **→ Game:** radio and billboard ads; a TV preacher whose "seed" hotline is a playable scam thread.
- **Youth-group culture.** Wednesday night worship bands with fog machines, purity culture hangover, ski trips to Colorado, "camp high" testimonies, youth pastors in skinny jeans transitioning to church-plant founders. **→ Game:** pedestrian archetypes + a church-plant startup run exactly like a tech startup (pitch decks, "launch Sunday," growth metrics) — startup satire and church satire in one.
- **The church-industrial complex.** Christmas productions with live camels and flying angels (Prestonwood's "Gift of Christmas" sells tickets like Broadway); worship-music royalty empires; church security teams (armed, ear-pieced) post-2019 White Settlement church shooting. **→ Game:** December world-event; armed greeter NPC archetype.

## 7. Food Canon

- **BBQ pilgrimage.** Goldee's (Fort Worth — Texas Monthly's #1, line forms at dawn), Cattleack (Farmers Branch, two days a week only), Pecan Lodge (Deep Ellum), Hurtado (Arlington, birria-brisket fusion), Panther City & Smoke-A-Holics (FW Southside, Tex-Mex-soul BBQ), Zavala's (Grand Prairie). Rules: brisket by the pound, sells out by 1 p.m., no sauce evangelism. **→ Game:** "line culture" as world detail — peds camp at 7 a.m.; a sold-out sign that actually rotates.
- **Tex-Mex institutions.** El Fenix (1918), Mi Cocina (the "Mambo Taxi" frozen swirl has a hard two-drink limit), Joe T. Garcia's (Fort Worth: cash-only, sprawling garden patio, functionally two menu items, always a line). The *frozen margarita machine was invented in Dallas in 1971* (it's in the Smithsonian). Tex-Mex is not Mexican food and locals will fight you. **→ Game:** margarita-machine origin = in-world plaque/landmark joke; patio restaurant as meeting-scene set.
- **Whataburger.** Orange-and-white 24-hour church; honey butter chicken biscuit after 11 p.m.; table tents stolen as dorm décor; the In-N-Out border war treated as a matter of state security. Pronounced "Waterburger" by many. **→ Game:** the fast-food loyalty war is a full ad-campaign satire on radio.
- **Buc-ee's.** Not a gas station — a pilgrimage: 100+ pumps, world-record car wash, immaculate bathrooms, wall of jerky, Beaver Nuggets, brisket sandwiches chopped on camera; billboards 100 miles out ("Only 262 miles to Buc-ee's"). **→ Game:** a mega-travel-stop parody is a destination POI; its billboard countdown is a running map gag.
- **Kolaches (actually klobasneky).** Czech Stop in West, TX (I-35 to Waco) is the road-trip sacrament; every DFW donut shop sells sausage-cheese "kolaches" and Czech grandmothers die a little each time. **→ Game:** highway pit-stop chain; pedantic-purist NPC bit.
- **Street food.** Paleta carts with bells in Oak Cliff, elote en vaso (mayo, Tajín, lime), taco trucks on Jefferson/Buckner (tacos de trompo — Dallas's spit-fired al pastor cousin is a genuine local specialty), birria everything, pupuserías (big Salvadoran community), Braum's (Oklahoma dairy chain, unreasonable devotion). **→ Game:** food-cart vendors as ambient economy + info brokers.
- **Deep-cut origins.** Fritos: invented in Dallas (1932). German chocolate cake: popularized via a Dallas newspaper recipe (1957). Dr Pepper: Waco-born, corporate HQ now in Plano. 7-Eleven: born in Dallas (Oak Cliff icehouse). **→ Game:** convenience-store chain parody has *local-origin* lore; snack brands get Texas-birth ad copy.

## 8. Money Strata

- **Old oil money.** The dynasties: a Fort Worth family that rebuilt downtown; a Dallas clan whose patriarch was once the world's richest wildcatter and whose sons tried to corner the silver market (1980); a computer-services billionaire's family whose heir built AllianceTexas (a 27,000-acre private airport-logistics city in north Fort Worth). Old money hides in Fort Worth; Dallas money wants to be seen. **→ Game:** dynasty families = mission-giver aristocracy; a private logistics airport is heist infrastructure.
- **Highland Park.** A separate town *inside* Dallas: own police, first planned shopping center in America (1931), Christmas-lights pilgrimage traffic, teardown mansions, generational SMU pipeline. Nearby Preston Hollow = billionaires behind hedges. **→ Game:** the wealth enclave with its own aggressive police jurisdiction — crossing the town line changes wanted behavior.
- **Southlake/Westlake.** New-money fortress suburbs: Carroll Dragons football, Town Square Stepford perfection, gated Vaquero, brokerage-campus fortresses. Median household incomes among America's highest. **→ Game:** HOA-perfect district where crime feels sacrilegious — maximum contrast value.
- **Corporate transplant waves (verified 2025–26).** DFW is America's #1 HQ-relocation metro — 100+ HQs since 2018, 11 net new in 2025 alone: KFC's U.S. HQ to Plano, Care.com to Dallas, Caterpillar to Irving, Goldman Sachs building a massive Dallas campus, Wells Fargo's new Irving campus, Toyota/Charles Schwab/Fidelity already rooted. Frisco adds a PGA HQ and a Universal kids theme park. **→ Game:** "HQ relocation announcement" as recurring radio-news satire; a relocating-corporation questline (tax abatements, ribbon cuttings, layoffs).
- **Private-equity landlords.** DFW is ground zero for institutional single-family landlords — the biggest one is *headquartered in Dallas*. Build-to-rent subdivisions, algorithmic rent hikes, code-violation slumlording at scale. **→ Game:** a faceless landlord corporation as villain faction — evictions, lockouts, a rent-drone app.
- **South Dallas disinvestment.** Fair Park: a world-class art-deco campus surrounded by fenced-off neighborhoods it helped displace; food deserts, highway scars (S.M. Wright's "Dead Man's Curve" removed only recently), Joppa — a freedman's town beside industrial plants; Tenth Street Historic District, a freedman's town being demolished house-by-house via code enforcement; Bonton's urban farm as grassroots answer. **→ Game:** the game's moral center — treat with specificity and dignity; missions about land, inheritance, and city hall, not street-crime clichés.
- **The servant economy.** The suburbs run on a pre-dawn shift: landscaping crews towing trailers up the Tollway, nannies on 90-minute commutes, day-labor corners in Garland and Irving, kitchen brigades from Pleasant Grove staffing Southlake patios, toll roads pricing the poor onto clogged free lanes ("Lexus lanes"). **→ Game:** traffic itself tells the class story — 5 a.m. work-truck migration north, 6 p.m. reverse flow; NPC schedules mirror it.

## 9. Communities (Respectful, Specific, Human)

- **Mexican-American Oak Cliff.** Jefferson Blvd: quinceañera dress shops, western-wear stores, the restored Texas Theatre (art-house now; infamous 1963 arrest site), Cinco de Mayo parade, lowrider Sundays, panaderías at dawn. Bishop Arts gentrification eats at the edges — taquerías replaced by $9-latte shops. **→ Game:** the most textured neighborhood on the map; gentrification tension as ambient storytelling (moving trucks, "coming soon" signs).
- **Vietnamese Arlington/Haltom City.** Legacy of 1975 refugee resettlement: pho halls, bánh mì bakeries, Asia Times Square (Grand Prairie) with its Tết festival, lion dances, night markets; Buddhist temples off stroad frontage; strong small-business culture (nails, seafood, jewelry). **→ Game:** Tết festival as seasonal event; family-business NPCs with real interiority, never accents-as-jokes.
- **Korean Carrollton.** Old Denton Road corridor — officially designated Koreatown by the state (2023): H Mart anchor, 24-hour K-BBQ, jjimjilbang spa culture, karaoke rooms, Korean megachurches, first-gen parents/K-pop-fluent kids. **→ Game:** late-night district (BBQ smoke, noraebang neon) — best 2 a.m. atmosphere on the map.
- **Ethiopian/Eritrean Dallas.** One of the South's largest habesha communities (northeast Dallas/Greenville Ave orbit): injera bakeries, coffee ceremonies performed for guests, Orthodox churches in converted buildings, late-night café-hookah debate culture, taxi/rideshare and nursing professions. **→ Game:** a coffee-ceremony scene as a "meeting with the elder" mission staple — hospitality as power.
- **South Asian Irving/Frisco.** Irving's Valley Ranch "Little India" strip malls (chaat cafés, sari boutiques, cricket on TVs), Frisco/Plano tech-professional wave, massive Hindu temples, Eid at the mosques of Richardson/Plano, Diwali Mela drawing tens of thousands to Fair Park, Major League Cricket sellouts in Grand Prairie. **→ Game:** Diwali fireworks event; cricket-ground POI; tech-professional NPC archetypes beyond stereotype (they're the suburbs' new majority in places).
- **West African DFW.** One of America's largest Nigerian communities (medicine, nursing, logistics, ministry): jollof battles at summer cookouts, Igbo and Yoruba association galas, aso-ebi matching-fabric weddings that fill banquet halls, Ghanaian and Liberian congregations, Afrobeats club nights that outdraw hip-hop rooms. **→ Game:** Afrobeats station + banquet-hall wedding set-piece; gala fundraiser as social-infiltration mission.
- **Refugee crossroads: Vickery Meadow.** Dense apartment district where dozens of languages coexist (Burmese, Congolese, Bhutanese-Nepali, Somali) — resettlement agencies, soccer leagues, halal groceries. **→ Game:** the "whole world in one square mile" district; soccer-pitch neutral ground for factions.

## 10. Institutions & Rituals

- **State Fair of Texas.** 24 days each fall at Fair Park, 2M+ visitors: the 55-foot talking cowboy statue ("Howdy, folks!" — burned down in 2012 and was rebuilt, a genuine civic trauma), fried-food arms race (40 new foods in 2025; Fletcher's corny dogs are canon), Big Tex Choice Awards, auto show, livestock barns, the Texas-OU football hate-weekend at the Cotton Bowl mid-fair, plus a real legal war over banning guns on the fairgrounds. **→ Game:** the single best world-event template — a month-long fair district with minigames, pickpockets, fried-everything, and a giant talking mascot begging for parody.
- **Gun shows.** Weekend circuit at big exhibition halls and Will Rogers Center: folding tables of ARs and jerky, private-sale culture, raffle wheels, tactical-Tupperware energy. **→ Game:** rotating weekend POI where the player's arsenal meets satire of the whole ecosystem.
- **HOAs.** Texas HOAs can fine, lien, and foreclose: lawn-height citations, flag disputes, board coups, Ring-camera vigilantism, "Karen v. Karen" litigation. Frisco/McKinney are the heartland. **→ Game:** an HOA as a literal faction (patrols, fines, tow trucks) — the funniest villain in suburbia.
- **Quinceañeras.** A full economy: dress districts on Jefferson, chambelanes rehearsing choreography for months, party buses, banquet halls, norteño bands, the father-daughter waltz. **→ Game:** escort-the-party-bus mission; banquet-hall set pieces; dress-shop storefront density in Oak Cliff.
- **Megachurch Christmas productions.** Ticketed arena-scale shows: flying angels on wires, live camels on stage, 1,000-member choirs. **→ Game:** December event; a heist during the pageant (camels loose on the Tollway) writes itself.
- **HS football playoffs.** Six classifications of December madness culminating at the Cowboys' stadium; town caravans on I-20; radio pregame for 16-year-olds. **→ Game:** seasonal traffic/AI event + talk-radio obsession material.
- **First Monday Canton.** "World's largest flea market" — 100+ acres, monthly, an hour east: antiques, puppies, ammo, MAGA tees, funnel cakes, handshake cash deals. **→ Game:** edge-of-map bazaar where anything can be bought — fence economy hub.
- **Weather as ritual.** Tornado sirens (everyone goes *outside* to look), March hail totaling cars (a body-shop economy), ice-storm citywide shutdowns, 40-degree temperature swings in a day, August as a boss fight. **→ Game:** dynamic weather with gameplay teeth — hailstorms damage cars, sirens empty streets, ice days turn highways into demolition derbies.

## 11. Crime Texture (FOR FICTION)

- **The DFW specialty: white-collar fraud.** This is the region's true crime brand. Legacy: the 1980s savings-and-loan collapse — Dallas was its world capital; the I-30 condo land-flip scandal (appraisal fraud, straw buyers, a chain of flips before lunch) is a perfect heist blueprint. **→ Game:** make financial crime *playable* — land flips, appraisal fraud, shell LLCs — the anti-GTA heist that's actually more Texan.
- **Affinity fraud (current, verified 2025–26).** SEC cases targeting a North Texas religious community with a fake options-trading fund ("guaranteed 3–5% monthly"); a $91M Ponzi run by DFW residents; a Fort Worth crypto-mining "farm" convicted as a Ponzi (promised cheap electricity and machines that never existed). Pattern: the trusted community insider — church pew, cultural association, prayer group — is the vector. **→ Game:** a "trusted brother" con-artist archetype and a crypto-mining-shed scam questline; victims are sympathetic, never mocked.
- **Megachurch money.** Embezzlement, opaque "housing allowances," retirement-payout wars fought in public (see §6). **→ Game:** follow-the-tithe investigation missions.
- **Street racing crews.** Takeover crews with social-media promo accounts, spotters on overpasses, cash grudge races at an outlaw strip, impound-lot politics, new felony statutes raising stakes (2025). **→ Game:** the player's entry-level crime ladder; escalating police task-force response tier.
- **Cartel-logistics fiction tropes.** I-35 as the fictional pipeline spine; stash houses in unremarkable exurbs; trucking, used-car lots, and cash-intensive businesses as laundering fronts; DFW's real logistics supremacy (inland port, alliance airport, warehouse oceans in south Dallas County) makes "freight" the crime economy's skeleton. Keep it institutional/genre-fiction — no real orgs. **→ Game:** the warehouse-and-highway economy is the mid-game crime infrastructure.
- **Other true-crime flavor (historic, fictionalize freely).** Bonnie and Clyde were West Dallas kids (buried in Dallas cemeteries); a 1970s Fort Worth oil heir stood trial in the era's most sensational mansion-murder case and walked; robocall/telemedicine-billing fraud farms; title-washing chop shops; catalytic-converter rings. **→ Game:** period radio references, a mansion cold-case mission, and ambient scam-call audio.

## 12. Vernacular & Speech

- **Core Texanisms.** "Y'all" (singular-ish), "all y'all" (plural), "fixin' to" (about to), "might could," "over yonder," "tump over," "coke" = any soda ("What kind of coke? Dr Pepper"), "tag" = license plate, "sir/ma'am" to anyone older, "bless your heart" (weaponized sympathy). **→ Game:** dialogue-writing baseline; NPC courtesy that curdles into menace is very Texas.
- **Highway grammar.** Roads by name: Central (75), Stemmons (35E), LBJ (635), the Tollway, the George Bush, Loop 820, the Mixmaster, the High Five; "service road" (never "feeder" — that's Houston); "Texas turnaround"; "FM road" in the exurbs; traffic reports as regional poetry ("stall on Stemmons at Mockingbird, both lanes of LBJ jammed at the High Five"). **→ Game:** radio traffic reports in authentic cadence = instant place-feel.
- **Place shorthand.** "The Metroplex," "Big D," "Triple D," "Funky Town"/"the Fort" (Fort Worth), "the Mid-Cities" (HEB — Hurst-Euless-Bedford), "the Cliff" (Oak Cliff), "the Grove" (Pleasant Grove), "Deep Ellum" (never "Deep Elm"), "the Shops at [anything]" for every mall. **→ Game:** parody names should echo these rhythms.
- **Dallas street/slab lexicon.** "Swangin'" (weaving lane to lane, slow), "comin' down," "elbows"/"swangas" (wire wheels), "candy" (paint), "trunk popped," "boppin'," "jammin'," "D-Town Boogie," "wassup wit it." Fort Worth adds its own drawl and "Go Yayo"-era bounce slang. **→ Game:** car-scene dialogue + radio-DJ patter authenticity.
